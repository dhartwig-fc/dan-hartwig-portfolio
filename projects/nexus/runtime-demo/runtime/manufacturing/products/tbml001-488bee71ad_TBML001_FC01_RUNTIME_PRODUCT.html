Alternative Explanations

  • Counterparty indicator (High): A counterparty scores high when the file on it is thin, not only when its behaviour is adverse. A newly onboarded but entirely legitimate supplier presents the same way as one being avoided: short history, few corroborating records, limited independent confirmation of who controls it. Settled by the KYC file, incorporation and beneficial-ownership records, and any trading history the group holds for the same entity elsewhere.
  • Inflation indicator (High): The benchmark is a comparable commodity unit price and does not adjust for specification, grade, warranty or bundled services, so a higher-specification variant sharing a commodity code will sit above it legitimately. Delivery terms do the same thing: a price quoted inclusive of freight and insurance is not comparable with a benchmark struck at the factory gate. A benchmark struck on a different date or in a different currency moves the variance without anything about the goods changing. Settled by the sales contract, the goods specification, the delivery term on the invoice, and the date and currency the benchmark was struck on.
  • Jurisdiction indicator (High): A corridor can be high risk in aggregate and ordinary for this trade. Routing through a major transhipment hub is a normal logistics decision for containerised freight and is chosen by the carrier as often as by the shipper. Settled by whether the physical goods movement matches the payment flow, and whether the route is commercially sensible for this commodity and volume.
  • Network indicator (Critical): Common directors and common ownership across entities is the ordinary shape of a corporate group, not evidence of a constructed one. Where two parties to a trade are related, transfer pricing conducted within an agreed policy will look like related-party mispricing from the outside. Settled by the group structure, and the transfer-pricing policy or advance pricing agreement covering transactions between these entities.
  • Trade value indicator (Critical): Transaction size is a function of quantity and contract shape. A single large shipment drawn down against a framework agreement is unremarkable for a corporate customer and will score the same as an isolated large payment. Settled by the framework or purchase agreement, and the shipment history drawn against it.

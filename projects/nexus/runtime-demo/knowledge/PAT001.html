PAT001 · Network Intelligence

Shared Director

Do two or more entities share the same director or controlling officer?

Supporting questions:

Is the shared director current or historic?

How many entities are connected through the same director?

Are any connected entities high risk?

Does the shared director indicate common control?

Does the relationship create related-party, sanctions or financial crime exposure?

NI001 Shared Director identifies entities connected through a common director, officer, appointed representative or controlling individual.

The purpose of this intelligence product is to detect relationships that may indicate common control, related-party activity, undisclosed connections or coordinated financial crime risk.

This intelligence product converts director relationship findings into a structured Network Intelligence Asset that can be consumed by:

  • Network Risk Assessment
  • TBML Analytics
  • Correspondent Banking Analytics
  • Capital Markets Analytics
  • Sanctions Analytics
  • Case Intelligence
  • AI Investigator Copilot
  • Investigator Workflows

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Shared director between customer and counterparty
  • Shared director linked to high-risk jurisdiction
  • Shared director linked to multiple shell companies
  • Shared director connected to sanctioned entity
  • Undisclosed related-party relationship
  • Director appears across high-risk network cluster

Investigation actions

  1. Same person acting as director of multiple entities
  2. Same officer acting across connected companies
  3. Same appointed representative across entities
  4. Historic director links where relevant
  5. Director links combined with high-risk indicators

Detection logic

NI001 should identify:

Same person acting as director of multiple entities

Same officer acting across connected companies

Same appointed representative across entities

Historic director links where relevant

Director links combined with high-risk indicators

How this pattern looks

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: one finding of fc-03's shared-director analytic over its own synthetic sample network of 20 entities and 20 relationships, and the records the engine read for it. Every person and company is fictional. The engine's rule is simple: a director with more than one DIRECTOR_OF record is a finding, and the engine then scores each finding with a network risk engine. Four of the five figures behind that score describe the whole sample network and not the director, and two of them are zero only because the analytic hands the engine nothing for them, so each step says only whether the engine's output holds a figure above zero: a zero is never read as clear. The records shown are the engine's INPUT, copied from its file; the engine's output does not return them. This page's detection logic asks whether a shared director is current or historic: the records the engine reads hold a confidence and no date and no status, so the engine cannot say. The engine gives no alert line. The subject is the director NEXUS's adapter chose, the one with the widest reach and the lowest id where two tie, not a choice the engine makes. This is the engine's own output for one sample director, not a customer case.

  5. Relationship risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives relationship risk 52.83 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 10.566 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleRelationship risk: the engine gives each relationship of the whole network a base figure by its type (ultimate controller 90, control 85, beneficial owner 80, trades with or transacts with 60, director of or shares contact with 55, shares address with 50, sanctions exposure 100, any other type 40; the engine's table spells the two shares kinds SHARES_CONTACT_WITH and SHARES_ADDRESS_WITH, so a record spelled SHARES_CONTACT or SHARES_ADDRESS reads as any other type), multiplies it by that relationship's own confidence and takes the mean over all of them, so many ordinary relationships dilute a severe one. The sample's 20 relationships are all DIRECTOR_OF (55), with confidences averaging 0.9605, so this component is 55 times 0.9605, which the engine rounds to two places, 52.83. It reads every relationship of the file and not the finding's own, so every finding of this sample run carries the same figure. A blank confidence value stops the engine, and a missing confidence counts as 1. Every relationship scores at least 40 times its own confidence, so this figure is above zero for any network with a relationship of some confidence: found here says the network has relationships, not that any is severe. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero

  7. Exposure risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives exposure risk 10 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 2 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleExposure risk: the analytic hands the engine the finding itself as its one exposure indicator, and the engine reads its text: the finding's own fields (the type, the director's id and name, the connected entities and their count), written out as Python prints a dictionary, field names included. The row the run keeps is that dictionary plus typology_id, typology_name and risk_assessment, which the analytic added afterwards, so the run holds the fields the text was read from. The engine's rule on that text: it adds 40 if the text contains SANCTION, otherwise 25 if it contains PEP, otherwise 20 if it contains ADVERSE or HIGH RISK, and otherwise 10 (capital letters are ignored, and a name that merely contains those letters counts: a name containing PEP would score 25), then 15 for each connected high-risk entity the network figures report, up to 45, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and report no connected high-risk entity. The text of this finding contains none of those words, so the engine adds 10 for it. That 10 is what any finding of this kind gets when its text holds none of those words. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the engine was handed something to read, not that anything about the subject is exposed

  8. Structural risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives structural risk 60 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 10%, which is 6 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleStructural risk: the engine adds three points for each node up to 30, one and a half for each relationship up to 30, ten for each level of ownership depth up to 25, and 100 times the network's density up to 15, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and no network size, ownership depth or density (it also returns lists of components, clusters, bridges, influence and exposure, which the risk engine does not read), so only the first two parts can score. This sample network has 20 nodes and 20 relationships, which the engine reads as 30 for the nodes and 30 for the relationships, 60 in all. It describes the whole network and not the subject, and every finding of this sample run carries it. The first part scores at least 3 for any network with a node, so this figure is above zero for any network with a node: found here says the network has size. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the network is that large, not that it is risky

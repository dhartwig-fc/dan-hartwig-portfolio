Third Party Payment Abuse

Is a trade being settled by a payer who is not the trade counterparty — an unrelated third party, split, or mismatched payment that breaks the link between the trade and the funds?

Third Party Payment Abuse settles a trade through a payer who is **not** the trade counterparty — an unrelated third party, split settlement, or a payment that does not match the trade. The payment breaks the link between the trade and the funds, letting value enter or leave under cover of a legitimate-looking transaction while the true source or destination of the money is obscured.

It is used to:

  • Break the link between a trade and the funds that settle it
  • Introduce or extract value through an unrelated payer
  • Layer funds through split or mismatched settlement
  • Obscure the true source or destination of money
  • Support related-party value transfer

Risk narrative

A customer's trade with a counterparty is settled not by that counterparty but by an unrelated third party, sometimes across several split payments. The trade documents describe a bilateral transaction; the settlement shows a different payer. The mismatch breaks the trade-to-funds link and lets value move through the trade without a traceable source. Where the counterparty is a shell and the parties share ownership, the third-party settlement is a laundering channel rather than a commercial convenience.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Third-Party Payer
  • Split Settlement
  • Payment Mismatch
  • High Value Trade
  • Related Party Trading
  • Shell Company Structure

Investigation actions

  1. Identify the third-party payer and establish its relationship to the trade.
  2. Trace the source of the funds that settled the trade.
  3. Assess whether the settlement breaks the trade-to-funds link.
  4. Resolve ownership; test for shared directors / owners and related-party trade.
  5. Package evidence for investigator review where the score is High or above.

Use cases

  • Trade Based Money Laundering (TBML)
  • Layering / Source-of-Funds Obfuscation
  • Related Party Value Transfer
  • Shell Company Abuse
  • Sanctions Evasion

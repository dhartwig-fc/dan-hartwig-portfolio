PAT010 · Network Intelligence

Hidden Controller

Who is actually controlling the entity or network?

Supporting questions:

Does a hidden controller exist?

Who benefits from the activity?

Who influences decisions?

Who controls multiple connected entities?

Does declared ownership differ from actual control?

Is the structure intentionally concealing control?

NI010 Hidden Controller identifies individuals or entities exercising effective control over an organisation despite not appearing as the declared owner, director or controller.

The purpose of this intelligence product is to detect:

  • Hidden beneficial ownership
  • Undisclosed control structures
  • Proxy ownership arrangements
  • Nominee director structures
  • Sanctions evasion schemes
  • Concealed influence networks
  • Financial crime control structures

NI010 represents the highest-value Network Intelligence capability because it seeks to identify who truly controls a network.

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: one finding of fc-03's hidden-controller analytic over its own synthetic sample network of 22 entities and 26 relationships, and the records the engine read for it. Every person and company is fictional. The engine finds a controller by two rules, one through ownership chains and one by counting a party's director, control, authorised-signatory and beneficial-owner records, and it calls a party a controller at three controlled entities. This page's doctrine lists nominee director structures and control without ownership: the engine's controller rules read no nominee-director record, so the nominee records the input holds for these entities are shown and those rules read none of them (the risk figure still counts them, at the default 40), and the engine does not decide that any control is hidden. It then scores each finding with a network risk engine; four of the five figures behind that score describe the whole sample network and not the controller, and two are zero only because the engine is handed nothing to score for them, so each step says only whether the engine's output holds what its rule describes, or a figure above zero. The records shown are the engine's INPUT, copied from its file. The engine gives no alert line. The subject is the controller NEXUS's adapter chose among the engine's findings: first one that nobody owns in the engine's input, because the engine's own comment says a holding company that is itself owned is not the hidden controller and the party above it is, then the one controlling the most entities, then the lowest id; the choice is NEXUS's, not the engine's. The finding on this page is the ownership rule's, which follows chains of ownership records down from the party nobody owns: the run keeps only the records that touch the case's own nodes, so the links of the chain between the subject and the entities it controls are not on this page, and none is supplied. This is the engine's own output for one sample controller, not a customer case.

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine's output holds it for the subject
  • not found the engine looked and its output holds none

  1. Control of several entities

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output holds a hidden-controller finding for John Cloverspindle (P001): it controls 3 entities, Delta Shipping Ltd (C004), Epsilon Commodities Ltd (C005), Zeta Logistics Ltd (C006). The engine's input holds no ownership record naming John Cloverspindle (P001) as owned, so nobody owns it in the engine's input and the ownership rule can start from it. The run holds this one finding's row; the case counts 3 findings. Over the same sample the engine's discovery rule also returned C003, which the engine's input has owned by C002, and P003, which nobody owns: of the unowned controllers P001 and P003, which control three entities each, NEXUS took the lowest id

    The ruleControl: the discovery rule collects, for each party, the distinct entities it has a director, control, authorised-signatory or beneficial-owner record against, and returns a finding for a party with three or more. The ownership rule returns one for a party nobody owns that holds 25 percent or more of three or more entities down its chains (a missing percentage counts as 100); it records the end of each chain once for each path, so a party that owns an entity directly and through another is listed with it twice and its count is the number of paths, not of different entities. It reads the record's type and its two ids, and for the discovery rule neither a percentage nor a confidence. Which of the two rules returned a finding is not in the run: the warm test re-runs the engine over the sample and shows that the ownership rule returned this one, and that the discovery rule returned C003 and P003. The ownership rule starts only from a party nobody owns, and the engine's own comment says a holding company that is itself owned is not the hidden controller and the party above it is. The step is found where the engine returned a finding for the subject as the controller. The subject is always one of the engine's own findings, so this step is found for every finding the walk shows

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine reached 3 controlled entities from John Cloverspindle (P001). In the run John Cloverspindle (P001) has 1 ownership record of its own, to C001 (the run holds no name for it), so every chain the engine followed from it starts there; the records naming the controlled entities as owned come from C003 (the run holds no name for it). A record that touches none of the case's nodes is not in the run, so a link between two parties that are not nodes is not on this page, and this page supplies none.   7. Relationship risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives relationship risk 51.51 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 10.302 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleRelationship risk: the engine gives each relationship of the whole network a base figure by its type (ultimate controller 90, control 85, beneficial owner 80, trades with or transacts with 60, director of or shares contact with 55, shares address with 50, sanctions exposure 100, any other type 40; the engine's table spells the two shares kinds SHARES_CONTACT_WITH and SHARES_ADDRESS_WITH, so a record spelled SHARES_CONTACT or SHARES_ADDRESS reads as any other type), multiplies it by that relationship's own confidence and takes the mean over all of them, so many ordinary relationships dilute a severe one. The sample's 26 relationships are 2 BENEFICIAL_OWNER_OF (80), 5 CONTROLS (85), 5 DIRECTOR_OF (55) and 14 of types that read as any other (40): 4 OWNS, 5 NOMINEE_DIRECTOR_OF, 3 SHARES_CONTACT (the engine's table spells it SHARES_CONTACT_WITH) and 2 SHARES_ADDRESS (spelled SHARES_ADDRESS_WITH); weighted by their own confidences the mean is 51.51. It reads every relationship of the file and not the finding's own, so every finding of this sample run carries the same figure. A blank confidence value stops the engine, and a missing confidence counts as 1. Every relationship scores at least 40 times its own confidence, so this figure is above zero for any network with a relationship of some confidence: found here says the network has relationships, not that any is severe. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero

  9. Exposure risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives exposure risk 20 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 4 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleExposure risk: the analytic hands the engine the finding's own indicator labels as its exposure indicators, and the engine reads the text of each: it adds 40 if the text contains SANCTION, otherwise 25 if it contains PEP, otherwise 20 if it contains ADVERSE or HIGH RISK, and otherwise 10 (capital letters are ignored, and a name that merely contains those letters counts: a name containing PEP would score 25), then 15 for each connected high-risk entity the network figures report, up to 45, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and report no connected high-risk entity. This finding's two labels contain none of those words, so the engine adds 10 for each, 20 in all. Unlike most network walks this figure counts the finding's labels: a finding with three labels would score 30. The run holds those labels. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the engine was handed something to read, not that anything about the subject is exposed

  10. Structural risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives structural risk 60 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 10%, which is 6 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleStructural risk: the engine adds three points for each node up to 30, one and a half for each relationship up to 30, ten for each level of ownership depth up to 25, and 100 times the network's density up to 15, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and no network size, ownership depth or density (it also returns lists of components, clusters, bridges, influence and exposure, which the risk engine does not read), so only the first two parts can score. This sample network has 22 nodes and 26 relationships, which the engine reads as 30 for the nodes (three each is 66, capped at 30) and 30 for the relationships (one and a half each is 39, capped at 30), 60 in all. It describes the whole network and not the subject, and every finding of this sample run carries it. The first part scores at least 3 for any network with a node, so this figure is above zero for any network with a node: found here says the network has size. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the network is that large, not that it is risky

TBML010 · TBML

Sanctions Evasion Through Trade

Is a trade being used to move value or goods to or from a sanctioned party or jurisdiction — maintaining a commercial relationship that sanctions prohibit?

Sanctions Evasion Through Trade uses ordinary-looking trade to move value or goods to or from a sanctioned party or jurisdiction. The trade documents describe a legitimate transaction, but a party to it — or the jurisdiction it touches — is subject to sanctions. Trade becomes the vehicle for maintaining a commercial relationship that direct payment or name screening would block. This is the most severe of the TBML typologies.

It is used to:

  • Move value or goods to or from a sanctioned interest under cover of trade
  • Maintain a commercial relationship a sanctions programme prohibits
  • Route sanctioned trade through intermediaries and free zones
  • Conceal a designated party behind a clean-screening counterparty
  • Support state-linked or proliferation trade

Risk narrative

A customer trades a commodity with a counterparty that is on a sanctions list, and/or through a jurisdiction subject to sanctions. The paperwork is consistent with an ordinary trade, but the exposure is direct: a designated party, a sanctioned origin, and a high-risk corridor. Where the counterparty is a shell and the parties share ownership, the trade is a sanctions- evasion channel — and the finding escalates to Critical.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Sanctioned Party
  • Sanctioned Jurisdiction
  • High Risk Jurisdiction
  • High Risk Corridor
  • Shell Company Structure
  • Related Party Trading

Investigation actions

  1. Screen the parties and jurisdictions against sanctions lists (UN, OFAC, OFSI, EU).
  2. Resolve ownership and control; apply the 50% rule where relevant.
  3. Consider a freeze pending review.
  4. Assess whether intermediaries or free zones mask the sanctioned exposure.
  5. Escalate as a priority investigation.

Use cases

  • Sanctions Evasion · Trade Based Money Laundering (TBML)
  • Proliferation Financing · State-Linked Trade
  • Related Party Value Transfer · Shell Company Abuse

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine rates it Medium, High or Critical
  • not found the engine rates it Low
  • not stated the engine's output does not hold it

  1. Sanctioned party and sanctioned jurisdiction flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the sanctioned party flag as true and the sanctioned jurisdiction flag as true. The row gives no party name, list, country or value, and it does not give the high risk jurisdiction flag the engine also reads

    The ruleSanctions exposure: the engine reads three flags the sample gives for the trade, sanctioned party, sanctioned jurisdiction and high-risk jurisdiction. Its own description is sanctions exposure in the trade. It screens no list, resolves no ownership and looks up no country: the flags are supplied. A true sanctioned-party flag with a true sanctioned-jurisdiction flag is Critical; otherwise either of the two is High; otherwise a true high-risk jurisdiction flag alone is Medium; otherwise Low. The engine returns a row only for a trade with a true sanctioned-party or sanctioned-jurisdiction flag, so Medium and Low never reach a row, and the high-risk jurisdiction flag, which the row does not give, cannot change the band of a returned row. The sanctioned-jurisdiction flag is also the sanctions flag of the jurisdiction component, so one flag counts in two components. A sanctioned-party or sanctioned-jurisdiction flag left blank in the sample's trades file reads as true: the trade is returned, and a blank sanctioned-jurisdiction flag also makes the jurisdiction component Critical. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 30% of the score: 27 points

  2. Trade value

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the value behind it: the row holds no transaction value, no currency and no date

    The ruleTrade value: the engine reads the trade's transaction value from its sample trades file. Its scorer names the figure as US dollars, and no currency enters a score. A value under 50,000 is Low; 50,000 to under 250,000 is Medium; 250,000 to under 1,000,000 is High; 1,000,000 or more is Critical. These are the engine sample's own thresholds, not any regulator's. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  3. Corridor and sanctions flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points, and the sanctioned jurisdiction flag the engine copies into it as its sanctions flag, which the row gives as true. It does not give the corridor flag, and the row holds no country and no route

    The ruleJurisdiction: the engine reads a high-risk corridor flag from its sample trades file and a sanctions exposure flag. In the engine's own sample path the sanctions exposure flag is a copy of the trade's sanctioned-jurisdiction flag, the one the sanctions exposure component reads, so a true flag makes this component Critical. A true sanctions flag is Critical; otherwise a true corridor flag is High; otherwise Low. A corridor flag left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer has no Medium for this component. The engine sees no country or route, only the flags. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  4. Counterparty risk band

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the label behind it: the row holds no risk band label and no adverse media flag for the counterparty, which it identifies by its reference only

    The ruleCounterparty: the engine reads the counterparty's risk band label from its sample counterparty file and an adverse media flag. In the engine's own sample path the adverse media flag is set to false for every trade. A true adverse media flag is Critical; otherwise a label of Critical, High or Medium (ignoring capitals and surrounding spaces) gives that band; any other label gives Low, including a label the engine does not know, which is not refused. A trade whose counterparty is in no record of that file is not refused either: its missing label reads as Low. The label is one the sample gives, and the engine does not check it against anything. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  5. Shell and shared-control flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the flags behind it: the row holds no shell company indicator, no shared director flag, no shared owner flag and no relationship

    The ruleNetwork: the engine reads a shell company indicator from its sample counterparty file and, for the customer's entity, a shared director flag and a shared owner flag, each true when any relationship that entity is the source of carries it. A true shell indicator is Critical, and the director and owner flags then change nothing; otherwise a true director or owner flag is High; otherwise Low. A trade whose counterparty is in no record of the counterparty file is not refused either: its missing shell indicator reads as true, so the band is Critical. A shell indicator left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer can take a common address, which would give Medium, but the engine never passes one, so the sample cannot give Medium. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  6. Prior alerts

    not found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the count behind it: the row holds no prior alerts figure and no SAR history flag

    The ruleHistorical behaviour: the engine reads a prior alerts count. In the engine's own sample path the count is assumed to be 0 for every trade, so the band is Low whatever the trade's history. A count of 0 is Low; 1 is Medium; 2 or more is High. The scorer can also take a SAR history flag, which would be Critical, but the engine never passes one. Engine band: Low. This component carries 10% of the score: 1.5 points

Common Contact

Do two or more entities share the same contact details?

Supporting questions:

Do entities share phone numbers?

Do entities share email addresses?

Do entities share domains?

Do entities share operational contacts?

Does the contact indicate common control?

Does the contact create additional risk?

NI006 Common Contact identifies entities connected through shared contact information.

The purpose of this intelligence product is to detect relationships that may indicate:

  • Common control
  • Related-party activity
  • Undisclosed relationships
  • Coordinated activity
  • Shell company networks
  • Ownership concealment
  • Financial crime exposure

Common contacts often provide strong evidence of operational connectivity between entities that appear independent.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Shared email across multiple entities
  • Shared phone number across counterparties
  • Contact linked to sanctioned entity
  • Operational connectivity indicators
  • High-risk contact cluster detected

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: one finding of fc-03's common-contact analytic over its own synthetic sample network of 16 entities and 19 relationships, and the records the engine read for it. Every company is fictional. The engine's rule is the value: it reads the email, phone and contact number of each entity, trims each and writes it in lower case, and returns a finding for every value that more than one entity gives. This page's detection logic lists shared email domains and its questions ask whether entities share domains: the engine compares whole values only and takes no domain out of an address, so two entities at the same domain with different addresses are not a finding. The engine reads no relationship record, so the director and shared-address records the input holds for these entities are shown and the contact rule reads none of them. The engine then scores each finding with a network risk engine; four of the five figures behind that score describe the whole sample network and not the contact, and two are zero only because the analytic hands the engine nothing for them, so each step says only whether the engine's output holds a figure above zero. The engine gives no alert line. The subject is the contact NEXUS's adapter chose, the one given by the most entities, not a choice the engine makes. This is the engine's own output for one sample contact, not a customer case.

  6. Relationship risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives relationship risk 47.68 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 9.536 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleRelationship risk: the engine gives each relationship of the whole network a base figure by its type (ultimate controller 90, control 85, beneficial owner 80, trades with or transacts with 60, director of or shares contact with 55, shares address with 50, sanctions exposure 100, any other type 40; the engine's table spells the two shares kinds SHARES_CONTACT_WITH and SHARES_ADDRESS_WITH, so a record spelled SHARES_CONTACT or SHARES_ADDRESS reads as any other type), multiplies it by that relationship's own confidence and takes the mean over all of them, so many ordinary relationships dilute a severe one. The sample's 19 relationships are 12 DIRECTOR_OF (55) and 7 SHARES_ADDRESS, a type the engine's table spells SHARES_ADDRESS_WITH, so each reads as any other type (40); weighted by their own confidences the mean is 47.68. It reads every relationship of the file and not the finding's own, so every finding of this sample run carries the same figure. A blank confidence value stops the engine, and a missing confidence counts as 1. Every relationship scores at least 40 times its own confidence, so this figure is above zero for any network with a relationship of some confidence: found here says the network has relationships, not that any is severe. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero

  8. Exposure risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives exposure risk 10 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 2 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleExposure risk: the analytic hands the engine the finding itself as its one exposure indicator, and the engine reads its text: the finding's own fields (the type, the shared contact, the connected entities and their count), written out as Python prints a dictionary, field names included. The row the run keeps is that dictionary plus typology_id, typology_name and risk_assessment, which the analytic added afterwards, so the run holds the fields the text was read from. The engine's rule on that text: it adds 40 if the text contains SANCTION, otherwise 25 if it contains PEP, otherwise 20 if it contains ADVERSE or HIGH RISK, and otherwise 10 (capital letters are ignored, and a name that merely contains those letters counts: a name containing PEP would score 25), then 15 for each connected high-risk entity the network figures report, up to 45, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and report no connected high-risk entity. The text of this finding contains none of those words, so the engine adds 10 for it. That 10 is what any finding of this kind gets when its text holds none of those words. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the engine was handed something to read, not that anything about the subject is exposed

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives structural risk 58.5 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 10%, which is 5.85 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleStructural risk: the engine adds three points for each node up to 30, one and a half for each relationship up to 30, ten for each level of ownership depth up to 25, and 100 times the network's density up to 15, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and no network size, ownership depth or density (it also returns lists of components, clusters, bridges, influence and exposure, which the risk engine does not read), so only the first two parts can score. This sample network has 16 nodes and 19 relationships, which the engine reads as 30 for the nodes and 28.5 for the relationships (one and a half times 19), 58.5 in all. It describes the whole network and not the subject, and every finding of this sample run carries it. The first part scores at least 3 for any network with a node, so this figure is above zero for any network with a node: found here says the network has size. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the network is that large, not that it is risky

Under Invoicing

Is a trade transaction being used to transfer value through invoice deflation below expected market value — understating the price to move value to the importer, evade duty, or settle an obligation outside the visible trade?

Under Invoicing occurs when the value of goods or services on an invoice is intentionally understated below their true market value. It is the mirror of over-invoicing: where over-invoicing moves value to the exporter through overpayment, under-invoicing moves value to the importer — the buyer receives goods worth far more than the price paid, and the difference is settled or realised outside the visible trade.

It is used to:

  • Move illicit value across borders under the appearance of legitimate trade
  • Evade customs duties and import taxes
  • Facilitate capital flight into an importing jurisdiction
  • Settle obligations off-book between related parties
  • Conceal beneficial ownership and control

Risk narrative

A customer imports goods from a counterparty at a price materially below the market benchmark. The trade documents are internally consistent, but the declared value cannot be reconciled with the commodity's benchmark price. The value gap accrues to the importer; it may be settled through a separate channel, netted against an unrelated obligation, or simply realised on resale at market price. Where the counterparty is a shell and the parties share ownership, the under-price is not a commercial discount — it is a transfer mechanism.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Invoice Deflation
  • High Value Trade
  • High Risk Corridor
  • Related Party Trading
  • Shell Company Structure
  • Repeat Activity

Investigation actions

  1. Reconcile the declared unit price against the benchmark; confirm the commodity,
  2. Quantify the value gap and establish where it accrues.
  3. Assess counterparty commercial substance; test for shell indicators.
  4. Resolve ownership; test for shared directors / owners and related-party trade.
  5. Trace the payment trail for netting, split or third-party settlement.
  6. Package evidence for investigator review where the score is High or above.

Use cases

  • Trade Based Money Laundering (TBML)
  • Capital Flight
  • Customs / Duty Evasion
  • Related Party Trading
  • Shell Company Abuse
  • Sanctions Evasion
  • Tax Evasion

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine rates it Medium, High or Critical
  • not found the engine rates it Low
  • not stated the engine's output does not hold it

  1. Invoice price against its benchmark

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the invoice unit price as 600 and the benchmark unit price as 1700, and the deflation below the benchmark as 64.71% (shown to two decimals). The row gives no currency, quantity or date

    The rulePrice deflation: the engine divides the gap between the benchmark unit price and the invoice's unit price by the benchmark unit price, as a percentage, and matches the invoice to its benchmark by commodity code; an invoice whose commodity code matches no benchmark has no deflation and gets no row. Its own description is invoice value below market benchmark, but it compares unit prices. It reads no quantity and no currency. It returns a row only for a deflation above 30%, so a band of Low or Medium never reaches a row, and an invoice priced above its benchmark has a negative deflation and is Low. A deflation of 10% or less is Low; more than 10% up to 30% is Medium; more than 30% up to 60% is High; more than 60% is Critical. These are the engine sample's own thresholds, not any regulator's. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 30% of the score: 27 points

  2. Trade value

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the value behind it: the row holds no transaction value, no currency and no date

    The ruleTrade value: the engine reads the trade's transaction value from its sample trades file. Its scorer names the figure as US dollars, and no currency enters a score. A value under 50,000 is Low; 50,000 to under 250,000 is Medium; 250,000 to under 1,000,000 is High; 1,000,000 or more is Critical. A transaction that is in no record of the trades file is not refused: its missing value reads as Critical. These are the engine sample's own thresholds, not any regulator's. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  3. Corridor and sanctions flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the flags behind it: the row holds no corridor flag, no sanctions flag, no country and no route

    The ruleJurisdiction: the engine reads a high-risk corridor flag from its sample trades file and a sanctions exposure flag. In the engine's own sample path the sanctions exposure flag is set to false for every invoice, so the sample cannot give Critical. A true sanctions flag is Critical; otherwise a true corridor flag is High; otherwise Low. A transaction that is in no record of the trades file is not refused either: its missing corridor flag reads as true, so the band is High. A corridor flag left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer has no Medium for this component. The engine sees no country or route, only the flags. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  4. Counterparty risk band

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the label behind it: the row holds no risk band label and no adverse media flag for the counterparty, which the row does not carry (fc-10's adapter takes it from the engine's input frame)

    The ruleCounterparty: the engine reads the counterparty's risk band label from its sample counterparty file and an adverse media flag. In the engine's own sample path the adverse media flag is set to false for every invoice. A true adverse media flag is Critical; otherwise a label of Critical, High or Medium (ignoring capitals and surrounding spaces) gives that band; any other label gives Low, including a label the engine does not know, which is not refused. An invoice whose counterparty is in no record of that file is not refused either: its missing label reads as Low. The label is one the sample gives, and the engine does not check it against anything. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  5. Shell and shared-control flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the flags behind it: the row holds no shell company indicator, no shared director flag, no shared owner flag and no relationship

    The ruleNetwork: the engine reads a shell company indicator from its sample counterparty file and, for the customer's entity, a shared director flag and a shared owner flag, each true when any relationship that entity is the source of carries it. A true shell indicator is Critical, and the director and owner flags then change nothing; otherwise a true director or owner flag is High; otherwise Low. An invoice whose counterparty is in no record of the counterparty file is not refused either: its missing shell indicator reads as true, so the band is Critical. A shell indicator left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer can take a common address, which would give Medium, but the engine never passes one, so the sample cannot give Medium. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  6. Prior alerts

    not found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the count behind it: the row holds no prior alerts figure and no SAR history flag

    The ruleHistorical behaviour: the engine reads a prior alerts count. In the engine's own sample path the count is assumed to be 0 for every invoice, so the band is Low whatever the invoice's history. A count of 0 is Low; 1 is Medium; 2 or more is High. The scorer can also take a SAR history flag, which would be Critical, but the engine never passes one. Engine band: Low. This component carries 10% of the score: 1.5 points

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's row identifies the customer as CUST001 and carries no counterparty: fc-10's adapter takes the invoice's counterparty, CP001, from the engine's input frame. It reads the invoice's counterparty reference only to attach that counterparty's risk band and shell indicator from its sample counterparty file, and does not carry it to the row; it reads the customer reference only to attach the customer's entity, through which it finds the shared director and owner flags.

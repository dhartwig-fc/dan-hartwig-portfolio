Suspicious Order Patterns

Suspicious order patterns are the family's purely order-flow-behavioural typology: quote stuffing, momentum ignition, and abnormal cancel/amend rates. There is no single rule that condemns a trading account here — the finding is a convergence finding: it lives in how abnormal the cancel/amend rate is, how intense the peak order-submission rate is, how far price moves after an order burst relative to the instrument's normal move, and how far the average order size departs from the account's normal size.

Article 16 of the EU Market Abuse Regulation (MAR) requires trading venues and firms to maintain effective arrangements, systems and procedures to detect and report suspicious orders and transactions. MAR Annex II and the accompanying ESMA Guidelines on indicators of manipulative behaviour name quote stuffing and momentum ignition explicitly as indicators of manipulative order-flow behaviour. CM010 operationalises both.

Risk indicators

Cancel/Amend Rate

  • Cancel/amend rate far beyond what genuine trading activity produces
  • Cancellations clustered in short bursts rather than spread across the session
  • Orders entered and withdrawn before any realistic chance of execution

Quote Stuffing Intensity

  • Peak order-submission rate well beyond the account's normal cadence
  • Submission bursts timed around key price levels or reference points
  • Order-book depth distorted by high-frequency, low-intent order flow

Momentum Ignition Signal

  • Price move after the order burst well beyond the instrument's normal move
  • Directional move immediately following a cancel/amend or submission spike
  • Move not corroborated by underlying news, fundamentals or genuine volume

Order Size Anomaly

  • Average order size well beyond the account's normal size
  • Size anomaly concentrated in the same window as the order burst
  • Order sizing inconsistent with the account's declared trading mandate

Investigation actions

  1. Resolve the trader, the instrument and the venue operator.
  2. Compute the cancel/amend rate (cancelled orders / total orders).
  3. Compute the quote stuffing intensity (peak orders per second observed).
  4. Compute the momentum ignition ratio (price move after the burst / instrument's normal move).
  5. Compute the order size anomaly ratio (average order size / account's normal order size).
  6. Score each of the four components against its band cut-lines.
  7. Aggregate the weighted exposure score; escalate at or above the threshold.

A worked example, step by step: one account of the toolkit's synthetic sample data, as the CM010 engine scored it, named here by its trader. Every name is fictional. The account shown is the one the run scored highest. The figures in the sample carry no currency and no date: the cut-offs below are the engine sample's own thresholds, not any regulator's. The doctrine above cites MAR Article 16, and MAR Annex II with the ESMA guidelines on indicators of manipulative behaviour. The engine does not decide whether any of them applies: it does not know whether any order or transaction was suspicious, and it sets its bands from the sample's figures alone. Two of its components carry the names the doctrine above gives as indicators (quote stuffing intensity and momentum ignition signal). Their cut-offs are the engine sample's, not ESMA's. The engine does not see an order, a quote or a price: it reads seven figures the sample gives for the account, sets four bands from them, and does not check that the figures agree with one another. A figure left blank in the sample file is not read as zero, and the engine's own 'is missing' refusal does not fire for it (the file is read as text, so a blank reaches the scorer as empty text): the engine stops with an error, so no account in the file is scored. A name the engine only passes through, left blank, does not stop it. This is the engine's own output for one sample account, not a customer case.

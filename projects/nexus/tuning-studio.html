Update Pack 05 · Detection Tuning

Stress-testing the alerts.

Every transaction-monitoring rule is a trade: catch more, and the queue grows past what investigators can work. The Tuning Studio runs the platform's real TM rules against a population whose truth is known, shows what a threshold, a segment split or a challenger rule does to alert volume, precision, recall and investigator capacity, and turns the chosen setting into a governed, evidenced change. It is built over ten weeks with a training programme running alongside, one concept per week.

Status · in build · weeks 1–5 of 10 complete · three decisions logged · every rate on this page is planted truth, not a SAR outcome
The problem

Tuning needs something to be right about.

A threshold can only be tuned against outcomes, and outcomes are thin: the estate holds a few dozen transactions and a handful of dispositioned cases. Real SAR labels also arrive only for customers the rules already flagged, so a missed case never gets a label and recall looks better than it is. Meanwhile the one value rule in the estate, high_value, is a flat £75,000 cut-off, which can't tell a large customer's routine payment from a small customer's fifteen-fold spike.

The answer — planted truth

A seeded synthetic population where the suspicious behaviour is put there on purpose, so every alert can be scored and every miss is visible. Turn a scenario's intensity down until a rule stops catching it, and you have measured that rule's blind spot. It is a stress test, not evidence of real-world performance, and the studio labels every rate that way.

How it works

One population, one loop, one governed exit.

The same deterministic population feeds the studio and the training notebooks, so what is learned and what is built are measured on identical data.

The blind spot, found on day one

The planted spike is relative to each customer's own level. A flat £75k threshold catches the big customers' spikes and misses the small customers' ones: a £30k payment from someone who usually sends £2k is a fifteen-fold jump, and it sits under the line. Segmented thresholds and behavioural rules (weeks 5–6) are measured against exactly this gap.

Why hold

A flat amount threshold isn't the lever: no setting of it is good, only differently bad. Moving it anyway would mean deciding what a missed case is worth, which nobody has stated, and tuning a live threshold to a population whose truth we planted. The real fix is a rule relative to each customer's own baseline, tested as the challenger in week 6.

Why hold — and what it costs

The recall a rule reports and the recall a team delivers are different numbers once the queue is counted. The owner kept the line and named the gap as a staffing ask (about 1,390 reviews a day at bank scale, on stated assumptions) rather than quietly trading coverage for a queue that fits. Until that is met, the drowned queue is the known state, and the log says so.

Why two lines — and why not yet applied

Compared at the same workload and scored on a population the lines were not fitted to, splitting retail from everyone else catches 27 more planted customers than one line. Four lines add five more, but refitting on fresh populations shows the thin lines swing by tens of thousands of pounds, and the one-case correspondent line switches itself off. Two lines keep nearly all the gain with none too thin to defend. The decision is recorded but not applied: the live threshold stays £75,000 until the governed apply step in week 9, which first has to teach the rule registry per-segment lines.

The training programme

The course is also the check on the build.

The studio computes its metrics by hand so the platform stays free of heavy dependencies. The training notebooks compute the same numbers with scikit-learn, on the same population, and a parity test requires the two to agree exactly. Learning the method and verifying the build are the same act.

The principle — the studio proposes, governance applies

The studio can preview any setting against the real scorer, but it cannot change one. A change goes through a logged rationale, a version bump and the full test suite. Calibrating to real outcomes still needs a labelled history the estate doesn't have yet, and the studio says so rather than papering over it.

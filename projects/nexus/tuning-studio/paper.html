Tuning paper: cash_30d, high_value

Generated from the Tuning Decision Log (8 decisions, sha256 ccfe1ad1c471aa9f), the TM rule registry TM-RULE-REGISTRY v1.0.0, and the independent challenges in challenges/. Every rate is PLANTED_TRUTH on a synthetic population, never a SAR outcome.

1. Background

  • cash_30d is proposed, not in the registry: nothing runs on it today (proposed line 15,000, D8).
  • high_value fires when a single transaction's amount is strictly greater than the threshold. Threshold today: 75,000 (registry v1.0.0).
  • Known limitation, as the registry records it: Compared on the raw amount whatever the currency, and flat across every customer: a large customer's routine payment and a small customer's fifteen-fold spike are judged by the same line. Measured on the Stage560 planted-truth population it catches 55% of planted value spikes.
  • Population: 5,000 customers over 12 months from 2025-10-01, seed 20260924, 4% planted value_spike (digest 7dc1ddbc19ba9e5e).

3. Results

D1 (week 3): HOLD

On the TM-SIM planted-truth population GBP 75,000 is dominated: it is not the cost-optimal threshold for any FN:FP ratio from 1 to 100, and the best F1 on the whole curve is 0.316 at GBP 187,500, catching under a third of planted cases. So a flat amount threshold is not the lever. Held rather than moved because any move is a call on what a missed case is worth, which nobody has stated, made on a population whose truth we planted -- tuning a live threshold to it would be fitting our own assumptions.

D2 (week 4): HOLD

Held at GBP 75,000 and the capacity gap named instead. On the stated assumptions -- 300 reviews a day at bank scale, TM-SIM a 1-in-150 sample (2.0 per working day), one alert per customer-month -- today's line makes 2,419 customer-months of work a year, 4.63x capacity. The queue ends the year 1,897 behind and effective recall is 0.135, none of it within 10 working days. The capacity frontier (GBP 177,500) would lift effective recall to 0.325, but the owner chose to keep the line and resource the team: holding it needs about 1,390 reviews a day at bank scale.

D3 (week 5): SEGMENT

Two lines: retail GBP 30,000, everyone else (business, corporate, correspondent) GBP 152,500. At D2's workload (2,419 customer-months a year) the two lines catch 149 of 200 planted customers on a hold-out population, against 122 for the best single line (151 vs 110 where they were fitted). One line per segment catches 154 on the hold-out, but corporate (9 planted) and correspondent (1) sit below the 10-planted floor, so four lines buy a handful more catches with two lines nobody could defend. At a 522 budget two lines (102) match or beat four (100). Decided, not applied: the registry stays at GBP 75,000 until the governed apply step, and it cannot yet express a per-segment threshold at all.

D4 (week 6): HOLD

Hold the champion at GBP 75,000 and advance the customer-baseline challenger to validation; reject amount-and-velocity. At D2's workload, on a hold-out population, the champion catches 122 of 200 planted (4.69 per 100 reviews). The customer-baseline rule (a payment over 4x the customer's own median of the previous 3 months) catches 170 (7.55 per 100) with less work (2,253 customer-months against 2,601), 63 of them ones the champion misses; its negative control shows no lift (0.9342). It is advanced, not adopted, for three reasons on the record: planted truth favours it by construction, it alerts 2.7x as many distinct customers, and 49 planted customers spike before they have enough history to be seen. Amount-and-velocity catches 88, every one of them a customer the champion already catches: it targets bursts, and the planted behaviour has none.

D5 (week 7): HOLD

Hold GBP 75,000 and name what drift does to each rule. Under the stated scenario (10% a year inflation, and non-retail business stepping up 1.6x from month 7 of year 2), with every rule held fixed: the champion's workload moves +73% then +124%; D3's two lines +48% then +85%; the champion indexed to the median payment +45% then +69% -- all past the 25% tolerance. The customer-baseline rule stays within it (+18%, +0%) because it is a ratio to each customer's own spend, but after the step its busiest month runs 1.74x its median month. PSI on payment amounts stays at or below 0.052304 ('stable') throughout: the input distribution barely moves while the tail that threshold rules live in does, so monitoring must watch each rule's alert volume, not the inputs.

  • Monitor each rule's OWN monthly alert volume against its first-period level, with the stated tolerances; PSI and KS on payment amounts read stable throughout while the flat rules' workload doubled, so they are not the trigger.
  • Recalibrate the flat lines (the champion and D3's two lines) at least annually. Indexing the champion to the median payment was measured and NOT adopted: it only halves the drift, because the drift sits in the tail.
  • Make a step-change warm-up part of the customer-baseline rule's week-9 validation: after a segment's business steps up, its monthly workload runs up to 1.74x its median month for about a quarter, the length of its baseline window.

D6 (week 8): HOLD

Hold the rules as they run and guard the cap. The champion and Decision 4's customer-baseline rule, blended 50/50 into one ranked score and worked to the D2 budget of 2,419 reviews, catch 166 planted customers in-sample and 171 on the hold-out, against 161 and 170 for the customer-baseline rule alone and 110 and 122 for the champion; it catches 0 customers neither rule catches. The gain does not justify governing a scoring model yet. It is weight-robust (amount weights 20-60 all catch 161-168) and its ranking is monotone on the hold-out (0 inversions), but the top band over-reads out of sample (45.6% planted in-sample, 34.9% at the same score edge on the hold-out), so a score must not be read as a probability. The cap is the finding: at a cap of 100 or above it costs nothing, because every month tied at it is worked; at 75, 3,375 months tie at the cap -- more than the budget -- and the label-blind alphabetical tie-break cuts recall to 112, the champion's level. In the estate, 6 of 48 entities sit at the macro cap of 100; the estate holds too few dispositioned cases to fit weights, so its preview shows what a change does, not whether it is right.

  • Watch the number of alerts tied at any score cap against the capacity that works them. A cap is free while every tied item is reviewed; once the tie exceeds capacity, the alphabetical tie-break decides who is reviewed, not the risk. Applies to the estate's macro cap of 100 as the queue grows.
  • Do not present a ranked score's value as a probability. The ranking transfers to unseen data; the score-to-rate mapping at the top does not.
  • Macro-score weight changes are previewed through the real scorer (tuning/macro_preview) before any governed change; no weight is changed on the estate's current three dispositioned cases.

D7 (week 9): HOLD, not yet validated

Hold GBP 75,000; the customer-baseline rule is not validated. Re-taken before commit on the independent challenge's evidence: the first draft carried the spike with a fallback forward as the candidate, compared only with the champion, and most of its fallback's extra catches fell in the simulation's first months. Scored against every rule the log holds, and again after a 3-month burn-in, only the champion and D3's two lines alone pass both stated conditions. D3's lines catch 149 on the hold-out, all attributable, against the champion's 122 (105 against 85 attributable after the burn-in), alert 1.30x the champion's customers, move +15% at worst under the volatility drift, and alert 15% of customers on pure noise. They are the candidate for the capstone. The spike with a GBP 2,000 floor and D3's lines as fallback stays the challenger to beat: after the burn-in it still catches 138 attributable against 105, but it alerts 2.91x the champion's customers, moves +55% under the volatility drift, and alerts 39% of customers on pure noise, so it fails both conditions.

  • Condition 1, volatility: at sigma 0.3 over three seeds, workload must stay within D5's 25% tolerance. Stated, not derived; the size of a realistic variance drift is unknown.
  • Condition 2, customers: distinct customers alerted must stay within 1.5x the champion's until the cost of a first alert is stated in its own unit (D4 challenge, point 2).
  • The candidate inherits D3's challenge: its gain depends on how closely the modelled segment tracks customer size, and its non-retail line is one seed's optimum. Both must be settled on real book data before any apply.
  • No live change: the registry has no rule kind for per-segment lines, and the governed apply step refuses D3 until it does.

D8 (week 10): PROPOSE

Propose a new 30-day cash rule, not applied: the finding is capacity, not a line. Re-taken before commit on the independent challenge's evidence. At the fixed 522 reviews a year, the team reviews only part of any line's alerts, so what it catches is decided by the order it works them in. Worked in signature order -- customer-months with the most deposits of GBP 4,000-10,000 in 30 days first -- the proposed line's queue catches 90 of 100 planted structurers on an unused seed, against 88 for the best rule that fits capacity on its own; with structurers who make fewer deposits, 52 against 53. Every plan tested lands in the same place at this capacity, so the engagement -- 20% fewer alerts with at most 5 points of recall lost -- cannot be met by tuning; it is D2's staffing gap again, now on a second rule. The line proposed as the target once capacity exists is GBP 15,000 (4,312 reviews against 6,207); it holds within the tolerance when structurers start lower or spread wider, but loses 8% when they make fewer deposits, and the figure is conditional on the planted pattern it was fitted to. The signature fails every stress and is used only to order the queue. On the same unused seed D7's candidate holds: D3's two lines catch 158 against the champion's 120.

  • Capacity: at the fixed capacity no plan meets the engagement; the D2 staffing ask now covers two rules, and the cash rule's share of the team must be stated before any apply.
  • Construction: the GBP 15,000 target and the signature's band are both conditional on the planted structuring pattern; neither may be applied without evidence from real structuring cases.
  • Scale: the population plants structuring at 2%, far above any real prevalence, which flatters every rule's catches per review.
  • No live change: the registry holds no cash rule, and the apply step refuses a new rule it cannot hold.

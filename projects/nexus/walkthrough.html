The problem it solves

Banks run two separate systems to fight financial crime: one to investigate — network and entity analytics — and another to manage the casework — alert triage, workflow, and reporting. Analysts swivel-chair between them, and the graph an investigator explores is never quite the one the alert was scored on.

NEXUS fuses both onto a single resolved-entity + money-flow substrate. The same graph you explore is the one the score was computed on, and the case is worked and dispositioned on the same page. Typologies and sanctions are overlays on that substrate — not separate products bolted together.

The two scores — the bit worth understanding

100
Macro triage score

Additive risk points across every signal, capped at 100. Its job is ranking — what does an analyst open first? Crans hits the cap: a closed loop, high-value legs, jurisdiction deviation, high-risk industry.

56 / Med
Typology analytic verdict

The weighted Circular Funds Flow model — six governed, tunable components. Its job is the analytic call: how strong is this specific typology on this entity? Medium, honestly — not force-escalated.

Two numbers because they answer two different questions. Conflating "what do I look at first" with "how strong is the case" is a classic detection-system failure — keeping them separate is the point.

Under the hood & what it demonstrates

How it's built

  • Real pillar data — entities, ownership, transactions, KYC, screening, shipping.
  • Resolved once, reused — the entity graph is shared across every typology.
  • Deterministic — byte-identical rebuilds, no hidden randomness.
  • Tested end-to-end — five-layer functional packs: network → analytic → score → alert → output.

What it shows I can do

  • The domain — typologies, entity resolution, sanctions, SAR, AMLD6.
  • The vendor landscape — investigate + manage, fused, not just named.
  • Full-stack build — data model → analytics → scoring → UI.
  • A governance mindset — provenance, tunable config, audit trail.

Known limitations & where it's going

The honest version — named before you ask
  • Entity-resolution matching is deliberately naive today; a production-grade resolver is the next replacement.
  • Single-source pillar data — no cross-source provenance fusion yet.
  • No beneficial-ownership / directors register yet — a known AMLD6 gap, already on the roadmap.
  • Score thresholds need calibration against real run history before they'd be trustworthy in production.

This is Beta v2.0 — the start of a proving journey, iterating in public. Naming the gaps is the point: it's a system built by someone who knows what "done" actually looks like.

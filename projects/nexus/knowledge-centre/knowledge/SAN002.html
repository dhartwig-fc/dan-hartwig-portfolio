SAN002 · Sanctions

Shadow Fleet

Shadow Fleet Operations involve the use of vessels, ownership structures, intermediaries and maritime logistics networks designed to facilitate sanctions evasion, conceal beneficial ownership and transport restricted commodities outside regulatory oversight.

The typology is commonly associated with Russian oil exports, Iranian petroleum shipments, DPRK sanctions evasion and other state-sponsored sanctions circumvention activities.

Unlike traditional sanctions screening risks, Shadow Fleet activity is often identified through network analysis, ownership structures, vessel behaviour patterns and trade route anomalies rather than direct sanctions list matches.

Risk indicators

Ownership

  • Common beneficial owners
  • Shared directors
  • Shared registered addresses
  • Offshore holding structures
  • Nominee ownership arrangements

Vessel

  • Frequent vessel renaming
  • Repeated flag changes
  • AIS transmission gaps
  • Unusual routing patterns
  • Ship-to-ship transfers

Jurisdiction

  • High-risk ports
  • Sanctioned jurisdictions
  • Weak regulatory oversight locations
  • Free trade zones

Trade

  • Restricted commodity movements
  • Russian crude oil
  • Iranian petroleum products
  • Dual-use goods
  • Military-related materials

Investigation actions

  1. Initial Screening
  2. Ownership Analysis
  3. Maritime Analysis
  4. Trade Analysis
  5. Network Review

Detection logic

  1. Resolve vessel ownership entities.
  2. Resolve vessel management entities.
  3. Resolve beneficial ownership structures.
  4. Identify links to sanctioned individuals or organisations.
  5. Identify ship-to-ship transfer activity.
  6. Identify AIS transmission anomalies.
  7. Identify high-risk port activity.
  8. Assess restricted commodity exposure.
  9. Calculate network exposure score.
  10. Escalate cases exceeding defined risk thresholds.

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine's output states it
  • not found the engine checked and its output states nothing
  • not stated the engine's output does not hold it

  1. Ownership

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output says the vessel's control chain, walked up from its registered owner, ends at a designated party at 100% effective ownership (the registered owner itself, counted at 100%). The chain has one reference, E-SILVERLINE: the registered owner is itself the designated party. The registered owner or the manager is itself on the sanctions list: yes

    The ruleOwnership exposure: the engine walks up from the vessel's registered owner, multiplying the stake at each step. A chain that reaches a designated party at 50% effective ownership or more is Critical, at 25% or more High, and below that Medium. With no such chain, a nominee link on the way up makes it Medium; otherwise Low. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 35% of the score: 31.5 points

  2. Designated party's record

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the designated party by its reference, E-SILVERLINE. It does not hold the party's name or the list it is on

    The ruleWhat lies behind the designated party's reference: its name, the list it is on and, for a chain of several references, the share held at each step

  3. Maritime behaviour

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output records 1 AIS gap, 1 ship-to-ship transfer, 2 flag changes and 3 high-risk port calls for this vessel. Its longest AIS gap was 37.5 hours

    The ruleMaritime behaviour: the engine counts four signs of concealment: an AIS gap, a ship-to-ship transfer, two or more flag changes, and a call at a high-risk port. None is Low, one Medium, two High, three or four Critical. A vessel with both an AIS gap and a ship-to-ship transfer is at least High. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 25% of the score: 22.5 points

  4. Vessel track

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's output holds the counts of AIS gaps and transfers, and the longest gap. It does not hold where or when any of them happened, or which vessel took part

    The ruleWhere and when the AIS gaps and the transfers happened: the positions, the dates and the other vessel

  5. Jurisdiction

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the vessel's flag as Russia. Port call in a jurisdiction on the engine's own list of sanctioned places: yes. Flag on the engine's own list of high-risk flags: no. It also records 3 high-risk port calls

    The ruleJurisdiction exposure: a call at a port in a jurisdiction on the engine's own list of sanctioned places (Russia, Iran, North Korea or Syria) is Critical. Otherwise a flag on the engine's own list of high-risk flags (Gabon, Cook Islands, Panama, Liberia, Marshall Islands, Comoros, Palau, Cameroon, Tanzania, Barbados) together with a call at a high-risk port is High; either alone is Medium; neither is Low. The flag alone, whatever it is, never makes it Critical. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  6. Cargo and corridor

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the cargo as Crude petroleum oils; Natural gas, liquefied. Across the vessel's trade legs, restricted cargo: yes. Dual-use cargo: no. High-risk corridor: yes

    The ruleTrade exposure: Restricted cargo together with dual-use cargo or a high-risk corridor, on any of the vessel's trade legs, is Critical. Restricted or dual-use cargo alone is High. A high-risk corridor alone is Medium. Otherwise Low. Each of the three facts counts if it is true of any of the vessel's trade legs; they need not come from the same leg. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  7. Manager

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output identifies the vessel's manager by its reference, E-SILVERLINE, and gives the manager's own risk band as High, yet the engine bands the manager Critical. By the rule below, only the manager's record flagging sanctions exposure does that; the output does not hold the flag itself. It does not show whether the record flagged adverse media, because sanctions exposure makes the band Critical whatever the media

    The ruleCounterparty exposure: the engine scores the vessel's manager only. A manager whose record flags sanctions exposure is Critical. Otherwise the manager's own risk band is used, raised one band from Low or Medium when the manager's record flags adverse media. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 10% of the score: 9 points

  8. Manager's record

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's output holds the manager's reference, E-SILVERLINE, and its risk band. It does not hold the two flags the engine read on the manager's record: sanctions exposure and adverse media. The engine reads no name for the manager

    The ruleWhat the engine sample's counterparty file holds about the manager besides its risk band: no name (the sample's entities file names the manager; the engine does not load that file), and the manager's sanctions-exposure and adverse-media flags, which the engine reads. The output shows only what the two flags do to the band

Marlin Crescent scored 90 of 100, Critical: the five components add to 90 points. 5 of the 8 steps found something; 0 found nothing and 3 could not be stated from the engine's output.

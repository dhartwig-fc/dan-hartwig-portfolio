Phantom Shipping

Is a trade being settled or invoiced for goods that were never actually shipped — a payment with no genuine movement of goods behind it?

Phantom Shipping settles or invoices a trade for goods that were never actually shipped. There is a payment, an invoice and often a contract, but no genuine movement of goods — the shipping record is missing, forged, or inconsistent with the trade. The trade exists only on paper to justify a cross-border payment.

It is used to:

  • Move illicit funds under the appearance of a genuine trade
  • Justify a payment with no underlying delivery
  • Layer funds through fictitious commerce
  • Support related-party value transfer
  • Evade controls that assume trade payments follow real shipments

Risk narrative

A customer pays a counterparty for a shipment of goods, but no shipping record supports the movement — there is no bill of lading, no transport evidence, or the shipping document is inconsistent with the trade. The payment is real; the shipment is not. Where the counterparty is a shell and the parties share ownership, the phantom shipment is a channel for value transfer rather than a documentation gap.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Missing Shipment
  • No Bill of Lading
  • Document Discrepancy
  • Payment Without Delivery
  • Related Party Trading
  • Shell Company Structure

Investigation actions

  1. Confirm the goods physically moved; obtain the bill of lading and transport evidence.
  2. Reconcile the payment against a genuine shipment.
  3. Assess counterparty commercial substance; test for shell indicators.
  4. Resolve ownership; test for shared directors / owners and related-party trade.
  5. Package evidence for investigator review where the score is High or above.

Use cases

  • Trade Based Money Laundering (TBML)
  • Fictitious Trade / Layering
  • Related Party Value Transfer
  • Shell Company Abuse
  • Sanctions Evasion
  • Capital Flight

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine rates it Medium, High or Critical
  • not found the engine rates it Low
  • not stated the engine's output does not hold it

  1. Shipping record and bill of lading

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives shipment present as false and bill of lading present as false. The engine derives both from its sample's shipping document file: the row does not hold the record, a shipment, a vessel or a date, and it does not give the document discrepancy flag the engine also reads

    The ruleShipment integrity: the engine reads whether its sample's shipping document file holds a record for the trade, whether that record carries a bill of lading reference, and the record's document discrepancy flag. Its own description is shipment-evidence integrity. It sees no shipment, vessel or movement of goods, only those records, and it reads no payment, so it cannot tell whether the trade was paid. No record is Critical; a record without a bill of lading is High; a record with a bill of lading and a true discrepancy flag is Medium; a record with a bill of lading and no discrepancy is Low. The engine returns a row only for a trade with no record or no bill of lading, so Medium and Low never reach a row, and the discrepancy flag cannot change the band of any row it returns: the scorer has answered Critical or High before it looks at the flag. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 30% of the score: 27 points

  2. Trade value

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the value behind it: the row holds no transaction value, no currency and no date

    The ruleTrade value: the engine reads the trade's transaction value from its sample trades file. Its scorer names the figure as US dollars, and no currency enters a score. A value under 50,000 is Low; 50,000 to under 250,000 is Medium; 250,000 to under 1,000,000 is High; 1,000,000 or more is Critical. These are the engine sample's own thresholds, not any regulator's. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  3. Corridor and sanctions flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the flags behind it: the row holds no corridor flag, no sanctions flag, no country and no route

    The ruleJurisdiction: the engine reads a high-risk corridor flag from its sample trades file and a sanctions exposure flag. In the engine's own sample path the sanctions exposure flag is set to false for every trade, so the sample cannot give Critical. A true sanctions flag is Critical; otherwise a true corridor flag is High; otherwise Low. A corridor flag left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer has no Medium for this component. The engine sees no country or route, only the flags. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  4. Counterparty risk band

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the label behind it: the row holds no risk band label and no adverse media flag for the counterparty, which it identifies by its reference only

    The ruleCounterparty: the engine reads the counterparty's risk band label from its sample counterparty file and an adverse media flag. In the engine's own sample path the adverse media flag is set to false for every trade. A true adverse media flag is Critical; otherwise a label of Critical, High or Medium (ignoring capitals and surrounding spaces) gives that band; any other label gives Low, including a label the engine does not know, which is not refused. A trade whose counterparty is in no record of that file is not refused either: its missing label reads as Low. The label is one the sample gives, and the engine does not check it against anything. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  5. Shell and shared-control flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the flags behind it: the row holds no shell company indicator, no shared director flag, no shared owner flag and no relationship

    The ruleNetwork: the engine reads a shell company indicator from its sample counterparty file and, for the customer's entity, a shared director flag and a shared owner flag, each true when any relationship that entity is the source of carries it. A true shell indicator is Critical, and the director and owner flags then change nothing; otherwise a true director or owner flag is High; otherwise Low. A trade whose counterparty is in no record of the counterparty file is not refused either: its missing shell indicator reads as true, so the band is Critical. A shell indicator left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer can take a common address, which would give Medium, but the engine never passes one, so the sample cannot give Medium. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  6. Prior alerts

    not found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the count behind it: the row holds no prior alerts figure and no SAR history flag

    The ruleHistorical behaviour: the engine reads a prior alerts count. In the engine's own sample path the count is assumed to be 0 for every trade, so the band is Low whatever the trade's history. A count of 0 is Low; 1 is Medium; 2 or more is High. The scorer can also take a SAR history flag, which would be Critical, but the engine never passes one. Engine band: Low. This component carries 10% of the score: 1.5 points

  7. The names behind the row

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's row identifies the customer as CUST001 and the counterparty as CP001, by reference only. The run's case, which the engine does not read, names them Solent Freight Ltd and Harbour Line DMCC. The row holds no name, address or country for either party

    The ruleWhat the engine does with the two references: it carries them to the row unscored. It reads the counterparty reference only to attach that counterparty's risk band and shell indicator from its sample counterparty file, and the customer reference only to attach the customer's entity, through which it finds the shared director and owner flags. It reads no name

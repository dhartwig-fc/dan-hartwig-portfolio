Phantom Shipping

Is a trade being settled or invoiced for goods that were never actually shipped — a payment with no genuine movement of goods behind it?

Phantom Shipping settles or invoices a trade for goods that were **never actually shipped**. There is a payment, an invoice and often a contract, but no genuine movement of goods — the shipping record is missing, forged, or inconsistent with the trade. The trade exists only on paper to justify a cross-border payment.

It is used to:

  • Move illicit funds under the appearance of a genuine trade
  • Justify a payment with no underlying delivery
  • Layer funds through fictitious commerce
  • Support related-party value transfer
  • Evade controls that assume trade payments follow real shipments

Risk narrative

A customer pays a counterparty for a shipment of goods, but no shipping record supports the movement — there is no bill of lading, no transport evidence, or the shipping document is inconsistent with the trade. The payment is real; the shipment is not. Where the counterparty is a shell and the parties share ownership, the phantom shipment is a channel for value transfer rather than a documentation gap.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Missing Shipment
  • No Bill of Lading
  • Document Discrepancy
  • Payment Without Delivery
  • Related Party Trading
  • Shell Company Structure

Investigation actions

  1. Confirm the goods physically moved; obtain the bill of lading and transport evidence.
  2. Reconcile the payment against a genuine shipment.
  3. Assess counterparty commercial substance; test for shell indicators.
  4. Resolve ownership; test for shared directors / owners and related-party trade.
  5. Package evidence for investigator review where the score is High or above.

Use cases

  • Trade Based Money Laundering (TBML)
  • Fictitious Trade / Layering
  • Related Party Value Transfer
  • Shell Company Abuse
  • Sanctions Evasion
  • Capital Flight

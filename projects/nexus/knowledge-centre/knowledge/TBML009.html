TBML009 · TBML

Shell Company Trading

Is a trade being routed through a counterparty with no genuine commercial substance — a shell used to give illicit trade a legitimate face?

Shell Company Trading routes trade through a counterparty with no genuine commercial substance — no employees, premises, trading history or economic footprint — used to give illicit trade a legitimate commercial face. The shell exists to transact: to hold accounts, sign contracts and settle payments while the real party stays hidden behind it.

It is used to:

  • Give illicit trade a legitimate-looking counterparty
  • Layer funds through a substanceless entity
  • Conceal the true party behind the trade
  • Support related-party value transfer
  • Facilitate sanctions evasion behind a clean-screening name

Risk narrative

A customer trades with a counterparty that has no real economic footprint — a low commercial- footprint score and a shell indicator. The trade documents describe an ordinary commercial relationship, but the counterparty has no employees, premises or trading history to support it. Where the parties share ownership and the corridor is high-risk, the shell is a conduit for value transfer rather than a genuine trading partner.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Shell Counterparty
  • No Commercial Footprint
  • Related Party Trading
  • High Value Trade
  • High Risk Corridor
  • Recently Incorporated

Investigation actions

  1. Assess the counterparty's commercial substance — employees, premises, trading history.
  2. Resolve the counterparty's ownership and ultimate beneficial owner.
  3. Test for a legitimate business rationale for the trade.
  4. Test for shared directors / owners and related-party trade.
  5. Package evidence for investigator review where the score is High or above.

Use cases

  • Trade Based Money Laundering (TBML)
  • Shell Company Abuse
  • Related Party Value Transfer
  • Layering
  • Sanctions Evasion

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine rates it Medium, High or Critical
  • not found the engine rates it Low
  • not stated the engine's output does not hold it

  1. Shell indicator and commercial footprint

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the shell company indicator as true and the commercial footprint score as 30. Both are values of the counterparty's record in the sample. The row gives no counterparty name, country or description of its business

    The ruleShell substance: the engine reads two values from its sample's counterparty file for the trade's counterparty, a shell company indicator and a commercial footprint score. Its own description is counterparty substance. It sees no business, premises or activity: the score is a number the sample gives. A true shell indicator with a footprint score under 35 is Critical; otherwise a true shell indicator is High; otherwise a score under 50 is Medium; otherwise Low. The engine cuts the score to a whole number before it compares it, which with whole-number cut-offs changes no band. It returns a row only for a trade whose counterparty's shell indicator is true, so Medium and Low never reach a row. The same shell indicator is also read by the network component, so one flag in the counterparty file counts in two components. A trade whose counterparty is in no record of that file reads as a shell and has no footprint score, which the scorer cannot cut to a whole number, and the engine stops with an error, so no trade in the file is scored. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 30% of the score: 27 points

  2. Trade value

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the value behind it: the row holds no transaction value, no currency and no date

    The ruleTrade value: the engine reads the trade's transaction value from its sample trades file. Its scorer names the figure as US dollars, and no currency enters a score. A value under 50,000 is Low; 50,000 to under 250,000 is Medium; 250,000 to under 1,000,000 is High; 1,000,000 or more is Critical. These are the engine sample's own thresholds, not any regulator's. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  3. Corridor and sanctions flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the flags behind it: the row holds no corridor flag, no sanctions flag, no country and no route

    The ruleJurisdiction: the engine reads a high-risk corridor flag from its sample trades file and a sanctions exposure flag. In the engine's own sample path the sanctions exposure flag is set to false for every trade, so the sample cannot give Critical. A true sanctions flag is Critical; otherwise a true corridor flag is High; otherwise Low. A corridor flag left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer has no Medium for this component. The engine sees no country or route, only the flags. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  4. Counterparty risk band

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the label behind it: the row holds no risk band label and no adverse media flag for the counterparty, which it identifies by its reference only

    The ruleCounterparty: the engine reads the counterparty's risk band label from its sample counterparty file and an adverse media flag. In the engine's own sample path the adverse media flag is set to false for every trade. A true adverse media flag is Critical; otherwise a label of Critical, High or Medium (ignoring capitals and surrounding spaces) gives that band; any other label gives Low, including a label the engine does not know, which is not refused. The label is one the sample gives, and the engine does not check it against anything. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  5. Shell and shared-control flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points, and the shell company indicator the engine also reads in the shell substance step, true. It does not give the shared director or shared owner flags behind it, and the row holds no relationship

    The ruleNetwork: the engine reads a shell company indicator from its sample counterparty file and, for the customer's entity, a shared director flag and a shared owner flag, each true when any relationship that entity is the source of carries it. A true shell indicator, the one the shell substance component also reads, is Critical, and the director and owner flags then change nothing; otherwise a true director or owner flag is High; otherwise Low. A shell indicator left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer can take a common address, which would give Medium, but the engine never passes one, so the sample cannot give Medium. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  6. Prior alerts

    not found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the count behind it: the row holds no prior alerts figure and no SAR history flag

    The ruleHistorical behaviour: the engine reads a prior alerts count. In the engine's own sample path the count is assumed to be 0 for every trade, so the band is Low whatever the trade's history. A count of 0 is Low; 1 is Medium; 2 or more is High. The scorer can also take a SAR history flag, which would be Critical, but the engine never passes one. Engine band: Low. This component carries 10% of the score: 1.5 points

  7. The names behind the row

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's row identifies the customer as CUST001 and the counterparty as CP001, by reference only. The run's case, which the engine does not read, names them Kestrel Trading Ltd and Transcaspian Trading Ltd. The row holds no name, address or country for either party. The engine's own sample counterparty file, from which it read CP001's shell indicator and commercial footprint score, names CP001 Onyx Holdings FZE

    The ruleWhat the engine does with the two references: it carries them to the row unscored. It reads the counterparty reference only to attach that counterparty's risk band and shell indicator from its sample counterparty file, and the customer reference only to attach the customer's entity, through which it finds the shared director and owner flags. It reads no name

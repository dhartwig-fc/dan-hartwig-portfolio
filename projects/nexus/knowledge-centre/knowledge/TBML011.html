Freight Cost Inflation

Is value being displaced out of a benchmarked or capped goods line into freight and insurance charges that are not benchmarked — so that the trade appears correctly priced while the real transfer happens in the ancillary costs?

Freight Cost Inflation occurs when value is displaced **out of the goods line and into the ancillary charges** on a trade document — freight and insurance — where nobody benchmarks it.

The goods line is the part of an invoice that gets scrutinised: customs reference it, price caps constrain it, and market benchmarks make an over- or under-priced commodity visible. Freight and insurance are not scrutinised the same way. A shipment can carry a defensible, benchmark-consistent goods price and still move substantial value through a freight charge several times the going rate for that route.

This makes it the natural successor to over- and under-invoicing. Where those manipulate the price of the goods, this typology leaves the goods price alone — often deliberately parking it just under a cap — and inflates the charges beside it.

It is used to:

  • Move value across borders while the goods line stays defensible
  • Circumvent a price cap without breaching it on the face of the document
  • Settle obligations off-book between related parties through service charges
  • Extract value from a jurisdiction under the appearance of ordinary logistics cost
  • Defeat controls that benchmark commodity prices but not freight rates

Risk narrative

A customer ships a commodity at a declared unit price that reconciles against the market benchmark, or sits just beneath an applicable price cap. The goods line raises nothing. The freight charge on the same document, however, is materially above the governed benchmark rate for that route and quantity, and the insurance premium is a larger share of cargo value than the route warrants.

Where the incoterm places the freight obligation on the *buyer* — EXW or FOB — a substantial freight charge on the seller's document is not merely expensive, it is inconsistent with the contract the document itself asserts.

The strongest form is compositional. A goods value pinned just below a cap, combined with freight materially above benchmark, is not two independent pricing quirks: it is one arrangement. The cap is respected on the goods line precisely so that the displaced value can travel in the freight line. That pairing is scored far above the sum of its parts.

Use cases

  • **Price-cap circumvention** — respecting a cap on the commodity while moving the excess
  • **Sanctions evasion** — extracting above-cap value from a restricted-origin commodity
  • **Capital flight** — moving value out of a jurisdiction as logistics expenditure
  • **Related-party value transfer** — settling intra-group obligations through inflated service
  • **Duty and tax evasion** — shifting dutiable value into non-dutiable service lines

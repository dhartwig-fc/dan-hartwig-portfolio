SAN010 · Sanctions

Remote IT Worker Revenue

Is this customer being paid for remote IT work on behalf of a sanctioned state, through an identity that does not hold up, infrastructure shared with other workers, and pay passed straight on towards facilitators?

North Korea earns hard currency by placing IT workers in remote jobs under false or borrowed identities. The wages land in ordinary bank accounts, often accounts opened for the purpose, and leave again within hours for exchanges, front companies and facilitators who pass the money back to the state. SAN010 finds the person or account through which that salary pipe runs, as a bank sees it.

The finding rests on four things a bank holds: whether the customer's identity holds up, whether the customer shares an address, phone or payout account with other unrelated customers, whether pay leaves as fast as it lands, and where it ends up. A laptop farm shows up as a cluster of high-scoring subjects with one address in common. The device and log-in data that would make the picture sharper are out of the first version, by owner decision, and are named below as a gap.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • An identity document numbered in sequence with another customer's document
  • An identity document issued on the same day as another unrelated customer's document
  • A customer name that is a close variant of another customer's name
  • A screening match at or above 85 to a listed North Korea facilitator
  • An identity document that appears on a North Korea designation
  • Five or more unrelated customers sharing one address, phone number or payout account
  • Ninety percent or more of payroll leaving to an exchange or a third party within 48 hours
  • A transfer to a digital-currency address on a North Korea listing
  • A deposit address one or two hops from a listed address on chain analytics
  • Onward payees in Russia, China, Laos or the United Arab Emirates

Investigation actions

  1. Pull the identity documents of every customer in the cluster and compare numbers, issue dates and issuing authorities.
  2. Confirm with the employer that the person paid is the person hired, through a live video check where the employer can arrange one.
  3. Map every customer sharing the address, phone or payout account, and review each one as a single case.
  4. Trace onward transfers to their exchanges and request beneficiary and deposit-address details under the travel rule.
  5. Refer a designation match to the sanctions lane at once and freeze as the regime requires.
  6. Consider a suspicious activity report for the cluster, naming the shared infrastructure and the onward payees.

Detection logic

  1. Screen every customer and keep every screening record; a customer with no screening record is refused, never scored as clean.
  2. Identity is Critical on a screening match at or above 85 to a listed party or on a listed identity document; otherwise High on two or more signals, Medium on one, Low on none.
  3. The identity signals are a document in sequence with another customer's, a document issued the same day as another customer's, and a name variant of another customer.
  4. Customers in one declared household are related and never count against each other, for identity signals or for shared infrastructure.
  5. Shared infrastructure counts the largest group of unrelated customers sharing one address, phone or payout account: five or more Critical, three or four High, two Medium.
  6. Pass-through measures the share of payroll leaving to an exchange or a third party within 48 hours of landing: 90 percent Critical, 70 percent High, 40 percent Medium.
  7. Payments to the customer's own accounts and to merchants are ordinary spending and never count; without any onward transfer pass-through is at most Medium.
  8. Destination is Critical on a transfer to a listed address, High one or two hops from one, Medium on an onward payee in a high-risk jurisdiction.
  9. A wallet with no chain-analytics record is refused; the hop counts are read from the same extract CMI010 uses and are never recomputed.
  10. Weight identity 30 percent, shared infrastructure 25, pass-through 25 and destination 20, and alert at 60 or above.
  11. A designation match goes to the sanctions lane whatever the score; the SAN010 score explains the hit and never replaces it.
  12. Log-in geography and device fingerprints are not used in this version; when the data dictionary carries them they join the identity and shared-infrastructure rules.

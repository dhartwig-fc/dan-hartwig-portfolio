TBML006 · TBML

Circular Trade

Do goods or funds cycle back to their origin through a chain of related trades — a closed loop with no genuine change of ownership?

Circular Trade (round-tripping) cycles goods or funds through a chain of related parties so that value leaves an entity and returns to it with no genuine change of ownership or economic purpose. Each individual trade looks legitimate; only when the chain is assembled does the loop appear — the same goods, or the value they represent, come back to where they started.

It is used to:

  • Inflate apparent trading volume and turnover
  • Move and launder value under cover of genuine-looking trade
  • Create fictitious commercial history for entities
  • Support related-party value transfer
  • Obscure the origin of funds

Risk narrative

A group of related entities trade the same commodity around a ring: entity A sells to B, B to C, and C back to A. Each leg carries an invoice, a payment and often a shipment, so no single trade is remarkable. Assembled, the trades form a closed loop that returns the goods and value to the originator. Where the parties share ownership and one is a shell, the ring is a laundering or value-transfer mechanism rather than genuine commerce.

Investigation actions

  1. Trace the goods and funds around the loop; confirm whether any genuine change of
  2. Establish the economic purpose (or absence of one) for the ring.
  3. Resolve common control and ownership across the parties.
  4. Assess whether any party is a shell without commercial substance.
  5. Package evidence for investigator review where the score is High or above.

Use cases

  • Trade Based Money Laundering (TBML)
  • Round-Tripping / Turnover Inflation
  • Related Party Value Transfer
  • Shell Company Abuse
  • Sanctions Evasion

How this pattern looks

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: one trade of the toolkit's synthetic sample data, as the TBML006 engine scored it, shown with the buyer the run's case names. Every name is fictional. The trade shown is the one the engine chose to stand for the loop it found: the engine draws a graph of who sold to whom, reports at most one closed loop (the first it finds), and returns one row for it, so the run holds no trade the engine did not alert. The engine does not see goods or funds moving: it reads only the sellers and buyers of the sample's trades. It reads ten inputs per trade: the length of the loop it found and nine figures and flags of the representative trade, three of which its own sample path sets to the same value for every trade. The run's row holds the loop length and the loop and the band, weight and points of all six components; for the nine figures and flags the walk can say what the engine does with them but not what they were. The figures carry no currency and no date: the cut-offs below are the engine sample's own thresholds, not any regulator's. A figure left blank in the sample files is not read as zero and is not refused: a blank transaction value reads as Critical, because a missing number fails every cut-off when its trade is the one scored; but when the engine picks the largest trade of the loop a blank value sorts last, so another trade of the loop stands for it. The engine reads no other figure from the files, so no other blank figure changes a row. This is the engine's own output for one sample trade, not a customer case.

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine rates it Medium, High or Critical
  • not found the engine rates it Low
  • not stated the engine's output does not hold it

  1. A closed loop of sellers and buyers

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the loop length as 3 and the loop as ENT002 → ENT001 → ENT003 → ENT002. The row also gives the one trade the engine chose to stand for the loop, with its buyer and seller. It does not give the other trades along the loop, their values or their dates

    The ruleCircularity: the engine draws a directed graph with one edge from each trade's seller to its buyer and looks for a closed loop with a depth-first walk that starts at the sellers in the order they first appear in its trades file. It reports the first loop it finds, so a file with two loops reports one. Its own description is round-tripping. It sees no goods, funds or ownership, only who sold to whom. The loop length is the number of entities in the loop. A loop of 4 or more is Critical; a loop of 3 is High; a loop of 2 or fewer is Medium, and a trade of an entity with itself is a loop of 1. The engine tells the scorer a loop was found whenever it reports a row, so the Low band for no loop never reaches a row. The row is the one trade with the highest transaction value among those whose seller and buyer are both in the loop, which need not be a trade along the loop's own edges, and the other five components are scored from that one trade. A trade whose customer or counterparty has no record has no entity on that side, and the engine does not refuse it. Every party with no record becomes one and the same node, so two different unrecorded parties can close a loop that does not exist, and a loop closed through that node either stops the run with an error or is reported as a loop through a party called nan, which of the two depending on the version of pandas the engine runs under. Such a trade is also drawn like any other, and because the engine starts its search at the sellers in the order they first appear in the file, it can change which loop the engine reports first. Engine band: High. This component carries 30% of the score: 21 points

  2. Trade value

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the value behind it: the row holds no transaction value, no currency and no date

    The ruleTrade value: the engine reads the trade's transaction value from its sample trades file. Its scorer names the figure as US dollars, and no currency enters a score. A value under 50,000 is Low; 50,000 to under 250,000 is Medium; 250,000 to under 1,000,000 is High; 1,000,000 or more is Critical. These are the engine sample's own thresholds, not any regulator's. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

  3. Corridor and sanctions flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the flags behind it: the row holds no corridor flag, no sanctions flag, no country and no route

    The ruleJurisdiction: the engine reads a high-risk corridor flag from its sample trades file and a sanctions exposure flag. In the engine's own sample path the sanctions exposure flag is set to false for every trade, so the sample cannot give Critical. A true sanctions flag is Critical; otherwise a true corridor flag is High; otherwise Low. A corridor flag left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer has no Medium for this component. The engine sees no country or route, only the flags. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  4. Counterparty risk band

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the label behind it: the row holds no risk band label and no adverse media flag for the seller, whose counterparty record (CP_A in the file) the row does not name; it identifies the seller by its entity reference only

    The ruleCounterparty: the engine reads the counterparty's risk band label from its sample counterparty file and an adverse media flag. In the engine's own sample path the adverse media flag is set to false for every trade. A true adverse media flag is Critical; otherwise a label of Critical, High or Medium (ignoring capitals and surrounding spaces) gives that band; any other label gives Low, including a label the engine does not know, which is not refused. The label is one the sample gives, and the engine does not check it against anything. Engine band: High. This component carries 15% of the score: 10.5 points

  5. Shell and shared-control flags

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the flags behind it: the row holds no shell company indicator, no shared director flag, no shared owner flag and no relationship

    The ruleNetwork: the engine reads a shell company indicator from its sample counterparty file and, for the buyer's entity, a shared director flag and a shared owner flag, each true when any relationship that entity is the source of carries it. A true shell indicator is Critical, and the director and owner flags then change nothing; otherwise a true director or owner flag is High; otherwise Low. A shell indicator left blank in a present record reads as true. The engine's scorer can take a common address, which would give Medium, but the engine never passes one, so the sample cannot give Medium. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 15% of the score: 13.5 points

    not found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives this component's band, weight and points. It does not give the count behind it: the row holds no prior alerts figure and no SAR history flag

    The ruleHistorical behaviour: the engine reads a prior alerts count. In the engine's own sample path the count is assumed to be 0 for every trade, so the band is Low whatever the trade's history. A count of 0 is Low; 1 is Medium; 2 or more is High. The scorer can also take a SAR history flag, which would be Critical, but the engine never passes one. Engine band: Low. This component carries 10% of the score: 1.5 points

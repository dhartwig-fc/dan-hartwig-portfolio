PAT003 · Network Intelligence

Layered Ownership

Does ownership pass through multiple intermediate entities before reaching the ultimate beneficial owner?

Supporting questions:

How many ownership layers exist?

Who is the ultimate beneficial owner?

Does the structure reduce transparency?

Does the ownership path cross jurisdictions?

Does the ownership chain create sanctions exposure?

Does the structure increase risk?

NI003 Layered Ownership identifies ownership structures where one or more intermediate entities exist between the ultimate beneficial owner and the subject entity.

The purpose of this intelligence product is to detect complex ownership structures that may be used to:

  • Conceal ultimate ownership
  • Obscure control relationships
  • Reduce transparency
  • Circumvent sanctions controls
  • Hide related-party relationships
  • Increase financial crime risk

Layered ownership is one of the strongest indicators of ownership complexity within a network.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Three-layer ownership structure
  • Cross-border ownership path
  • Offshore holding company
  • Reduced ownership transparency
  • Potential ownership concealment

Investigation actions

  1. Ownership chains greater than one layer
  2. Multi-jurisdiction ownership structures
  3. Complex holding company arrangements
  4. Nested ownership structures
  5. Cross-border ownership chains
  6. Ownership networks designed to obscure control

Detection logic

NI003 should identify:

Ownership chains greater than one layer

Multi-jurisdiction ownership structures

Complex holding company arrangements

Nested ownership structures

Cross-border ownership chains

Ownership networks designed to obscure control

How this pattern looks

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: one ownership chain returned by fc-03's layered-ownership analytic over its own synthetic sample network of 20 entities and 20 ownership records, and the records the engine read along it. Every person and company is fictional. The engine's rule is the chain's length: it walks down from each party that nobody owns and returns a finding for each chain of three links or more that ends at a party owning nothing further (a chain over ten links, or one running into a ring, returns nothing), with the ids in order and its own discovery label, which it fixes from the length alone. This page's detection logic also lists multi-jurisdiction and cross-border chains and says that layered ownership is one of the strongest indicators of complexity: the engine reads no country, and it does not decide whether a chain is concealment. It works out an effective ownership percentage along each chain and the finding does not return it, so none is shown here. The engine then scores each finding with a network risk engine; four of the five figures behind that score describe the whole sample network and not the chain, and two are zero only because the analytic hands the engine nothing for them, so each step says only whether the engine's output holds a figure above zero. Three words are kept apart on this page: the engine's discovery label for the chain, the engine's level word for its score, and the band NEXUS publishes. The engine gives no alert line. The subject is the company at the foot of the chain NEXUS's adapter chose, the one with the most parties and the lowest foot id where chains tie, not a choice the engine makes. This is the engine's own output for one sample chain, not a customer case.

Global Trading Ltd · C001 · foot of a chain of 4 links · the engine's score 15.71 of 100, its level Very Low · published band Low

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine's output holds it for the subject
  • not found the engine looked and its output holds none
  • not stated the engine's output does not say, or its figure is zero here

  1. Layered ownership chain

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output holds an ownership chain of 4 links, from Alexander Cloverspindle (P001) down to Global Trading Ltd (C001): Alexander Cloverspindle (P001), then Alpha Holdings Ltd (H001), then Beta Holdings Ltd (H002), then Gamma Holdings Ltd (H003), then Global Trading Ltd (C001). Global Trading Ltd (C001) is the foot of the chain, which NEXUS's adapter names the subject

    The ruleLayered ownership: the engine builds a graph from every record of an ownership type (owns, partial owner, shareholder, beneficial owner, ultimate owner, control) from the owner to the owned, walks down from each party that nobody owns, and returns a finding for each chain whose depth, counted in links, is 3 or more and that ends at a party owning nothing further: the ids in order and the depth. A stretch that stops short of such a party is not a finding of its own, a chain of more than 10 links is dropped (10 is returned, 11 is not), and a chain that runs into a ring returns nothing. It reads the record's type and its two ids to find a chain and not its confidence or any country. The step is found where the engine returned a chain with the subject at its foot. The subject is always one of the engine's own findings, so this step is found for every finding the walk shows

    What the engine statesThe engine names Alexander Cloverspindle (P001) as the ultimate owner of the chain. The engine's input holds no ownership record that names it as the owned party, and 2 records that name it as the owner

    The ruleTop of the chain: the engine starts only from a party that is nobody's owned party, so a holding company in the middle of a chain is not reported as the owner of the chain beneath it. The step is found where the engine's row names the top party and the input holds no ownership record that names it as owned. The subject is always one of the engine's own findings, so this step is found for every finding the walk shows

The engine works an effective percentage out from the percentages along a chain; the finding does not return it

    The ruleRecords along the chain: the engine reads each hop from a record of an ownership type, taking a missing percentage as 100 and a missing confidence as 1, and multiplies the percentages along a chain into an effective percentage. The finding carries the ids only. The records shown are what the engine read for each hop: they are not something it found, and this page works out no effective percentage. The step is found where the input holds a record for every hop of the chain. The subject is always one of the engine's own findings, so this step is found for every finding the walk shows

  4. Depth of the chain

    found

    What the engine statesThe chain is 4 links long, with 3 intermediate parties between the owner and the foot. The engine returns a chain at 3 links or more and gave this one its own discovery label High, which follows from the length alone

    The ruleDepth: the depth is the number of links, which is the number of parties minus one, so a chain of 3 links has two intermediate parties. The engine returns a chain at 3 links or more and sets its own discovery label by the same test: High at 3 or more, and Medium below, which it cannot return at its default threshold of 3. That label is the engine's discovery label for the chain: it is not the engine's risk level for the finding, which is below, and not the band NEXUS publishes. The step is found for any chain the engine returns, because every returned chain is at least 3 links

The step is not found where the input holds no such record and not stated where it does, because the run holds this one chain's row

    not stated

    The ruleEntity risk: the engine reads a risk score from each entity record of the whole network it is given, a record with no such column reading as 0 (a blank value stops the engine), and takes the mean over all entities. The toolkit's entity file for this sample has no risk score column, so every entity reads 0 and this component is 0 whoever the entities are. It describes the whole network and not the subject, and every finding of this sample run carries the same figure. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A zero here means nothing was supplied, not that the entities are clean

  7. Relationship risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives relationship risk 38.56 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 7.712 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleRelationship risk: the engine gives each relationship of the whole network a base figure by its type (ultimate controller 90, control 85, beneficial owner 80, trades with or transacts with 60, director of or shares contact with 55, shares address with 50, sanctions exposure 100, any other type 40; the engine's table spells the two shares kinds SHARES_CONTACT_WITH and SHARES_ADDRESS_WITH, so a record spelled SHARES_CONTACT or SHARES_ADDRESS reads as any other type), multiplies it by that relationship's own confidence and takes the mean over all of them, so many ordinary relationships dilute a severe one. The sample's 20 relationships are all OWNS, a type the engine's table does not list, so each reads as any other type (40), with confidences averaging 0.964, so this component is 40 times 0.964, which the engine rounds to two places, 38.56. Ownership links, the subject of this analytic, score lower than any listed type. It reads every relationship of the file and not the finding's own, so every finding of this sample run carries the same figure. A blank confidence value stops the engine, and a missing confidence counts as 1. Every relationship scores at least 40 times its own confidence, so this figure is above zero for any network with a relationship of some confidence: found here says the network has relationships, not that any is severe. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero

    The ruleOwnership risk: the engine scores each ownership path it is handed from its depth (20 for each level, up to 60), its effective ownership percentage (25 more at 50 or above, 15 at 25 or above) and its confidence. The analytic hands the engine no list of ownership paths, so this component is 0 in every run of this analytic, although the finding itself is an ownership chain: the chain is not handed over as a path to score.   9. Exposure risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives exposure risk 10 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 2 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleExposure risk: the analytic hands the engine the finding itself as its one exposure indicator, and the engine reads its text: the finding's own fields (the owner's id, the chain's ids in order, its depth, the discovery label and the typology tag), written out as Python prints a dictionary, field names included. The row the run keeps is that dictionary plus typology_id, typology_name and risk_assessment, which the analytic added afterwards, so the run holds the fields the text was read from. The engine's rule on that text: it adds 40 if the text contains SANCTION, otherwise 25 if it contains PEP, otherwise 20 if it contains ADVERSE or HIGH RISK, and otherwise 10 (capital letters are ignored, and a name that merely contains those letters counts: a name containing PEP would score 25), then 15 for each connected high-risk entity the network figures report, up to 45, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and report no connected high-risk entity. The text of this finding contains none of those words, so the engine adds 10 for it. That 10 is what any finding of this kind gets when its text holds none of those words. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the engine was handed something to read, not that anything about the subject is exposed

  10. Structural risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives structural risk 60 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 10%, which is 6 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleStructural risk: the engine adds three points for each node up to 30, one and a half for each relationship up to 30, ten for each level of ownership depth up to 25, and 100 times the network's density up to 15, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and no network size, ownership depth or density (it also returns lists of components, clusters, bridges, influence and exposure, which the risk engine does not read), so only the first two parts can score. This sample network has 20 nodes and 20 relationships, which the engine reads as 30 for the nodes and 30 for the relationships, 60 in all. It describes the whole network and not the subject, and every finding of this sample run carries it. The first part scores at least 3 for any network with a node, so this figure is above zero for any network with a node: found here says the network has size. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the network is that large, not that it is risky

The drivers about relationship types describe the whole network, not the subject. The step is found where the engine's list holds a driver and not found where it is empty

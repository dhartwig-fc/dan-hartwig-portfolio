BA004 · Correspondent Banking

Circular Funds Flow

A circular funds flow is a round-tripping loop: funds leave an originating account, pass through one or more intermediary accounts, and return to the same origin (or an account under the same control) having taken on the appearance of unrelated third-party activity along the way. It is BA003 Mule Accounts run backwards — where a mule forwards funds onward and never sees them again, a circular flow is engineered so the ORIGINATOR gets the funds back, laundered through the round trip itself.

The finding lives in four places: how much of what left the origin actually comes back to it, how many hops the loop takes to disguise the trip, how little value is lost along the way (ordinary correspondent banking bleeds fees and FX spread at every hop; a loop that returns almost lossless is not behaving like trade finance), and how quickly the whole round trip completes.

Risk indicators

Loop Closure

  • Funds returning to the origin account within the same reporting cycle they left it
  • Balance movements that net to near-zero over the round trip despite large gross flows

Hop Count

  • Multiple intermediary accounts carrying the funds between origin and return
  • Intermediaries with no independent commercial rationale for holding the funds

Value Preservation

  • Amounts moving hop to hop with negligible fee or FX erosion
  • Round-number consistency across legs inconsistent with genuine trade settlement

Timing Compression

  • The full round trip completing in hours or a few days
  • Legs scheduled back-to-back rather than at a pace consistent with real commercial timing

Investigation actions

  1. Resolve the originating account and every loop member (first hop, second hop, closure).
  2. Compute the loop closure ratio (returned amount / originated amount).
  3. Count the intermediary accounts (hops) the loop passes through before closing.
  4. Compute the average hop retention percentage (value preserved hop to hop).
  5. Compute the loop duration in hours from origination to return.
  6. Score each of the four components against its band cut-lines.
  7. Aggregate the weighted exposure score; escalate at or above the threshold.

    The ruleLoop closure: the engine divides the sample's returned amount by its originated amount, both as the sample gives them. Its own description is how much of what left the origin account comes back to it; it does not see the legs of the loop, or whether the returned amount reached the origin account. An originated amount of zero or less is refused, and the engine then scores no account in the file. Under 50% is Low; 50% to under 75% is Medium; 75% to under 92% is High; 92% or more is Critical. The row shows both amounts cut to whole numbers; the band is set from the sample's figures before that cut. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 35% of the score: 31.5 points

Engine band: Critical. This component carries 25% of the score: 22.5 points

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the figure the sample labels average hop retention as 0.985. It does not hold any hop, and the engine does not check the figure against the originated and returned amounts

    The ruleValue preservation: the engine reads one figure as the sample gives it, which the sample labels average hop retention and the engine describes as how little value is lost hop to hop across the loop. It does not see any hop. It compares the figure with cut-offs written as fractions of 1, and a higher figure is the worse: under 0.80 is Low; 0.80 to under 0.90 is Medium; 0.90 to under 0.97 is High; 0.97 or more is Critical.

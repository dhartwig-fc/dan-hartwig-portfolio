SAN006 · Sanctions

Trade Based Sanctions Evasion

Trade-Based Sanctions Evasion moves value or prohibited goods across a sanctioned boundary by manipulating the trade transaction itself — mispricing an invoice, or misdescribing the goods so that a controlled item travels under an innocuous label. The transaction is the instrument of evasion.

The finding is a trade-anomaly finding: an abnormal deviation between declared and benchmark price, and/or a mismatch between declared and actual goods, weighted against the destination, the counterparty and any ownership link to a designated party.

Risk indicators

Trade-Anomaly

  • Large price deviation from benchmark (over/under-pricing)
  • Goods misdescription (declared ≠ actual)
  • High-value cargo under generic descriptions

Jurisdiction

  • Sanctioned destination

Counterparty

  • Counterparty sanctions exposure or adverse media

Ownership

  • Trading party owned by / linked to a designated party

Investigation actions

  1. Resolve the trade, its parties and counterparties.
  2. Compare declared unit price against benchmark; grade the deviation.
  3. Compare declared against actual goods for misdescription.
  4. Score jurisdiction exposure for the destination.
  5. Score counterparty exposure and any ownership link to a designated party.
  6. Aggregate; escalate at or above the threshold.

Detection logic

  1. Resolve the trade, its parties and counterparties.
  2. Compare declared unit price against benchmark; grade the deviation.
  3. Compare declared against actual goods for misdescription.
  4. Score jurisdiction exposure for the destination.
  5. Score counterparty exposure and any ownership link to a designated party.
  6. Aggregate; escalate at or above the threshold.

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine's output states it
  • not found the engine checked and its output states nothing
  • not stated the engine's output does not hold it

  1. Price and goods

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the trade's declared unit price as 3000 and the benchmark unit price as 1500 (the row gives no currency or unit), a difference of 100% of the benchmark. It gives the goods declared as Textiles and the actual goods as Precision machine components, and states that the two descriptions differ

    The ruleTrade anomaly: the engine compares the declared unit price with the benchmark unit price, above or below, and the declared goods with the actual goods, ignoring capital letters. A price difference of 50% or more together with goods descriptions that differ is Critical; otherwise a difference of 50% or more, or of 25% or more together with descriptions that differ, is High; otherwise a difference of 25% or more, or descriptions that differ, is Medium; otherwise Low. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 40% of the score: 36 points

  2. Counterparty

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output identifies the counterparty by its reference, CP1, and gives the counterparty's own risk band as High

    The ruleCounterparty exposure: a counterparty whose record flags sanctions exposure is Critical; otherwise the counterparty's own risk band, raised one band from Low or Medium when its record flags adverse media. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 20% of the score: 18 points

  3. Counterparty's record

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's output holds the counterparty's reference, CP1, its own risk band, High, and the engine's band for it, Critical. The model raises a counterparty to Critical only for sanctions exposure, so the engine's output shows that the counterparty's record flagged sanctions exposure for CP1. It does not show whether the record flagged adverse media, because sanctions exposure makes the band Critical whatever the media. It does not hold the counterparty's name or the record itself

    The ruleWhat the engine sample's counterparty file holds besides its risk band: the counterparty's name, which the engine does not read, and its sanctions-exposure and adverse-media flags, which it does. The output shows only what the two flags do to the band

  4. Destination

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives the trade's destination as Iran. It states that this is on the engine's own list of sanctioned places and not on its own list of high-risk places

    The ruleJurisdiction exposure: a destination on the engine's own list of sanctioned places is Critical; otherwise a destination on its own list of high-risk places is High; otherwise Low. A destination counts only when it is named exactly as on a list, apart from surrounding spaces. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 20% of the score: 18 points

  5. Counterparty's owner

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output identifies the counterparty's owner by its reference, EDES1, and states that the owner is on the engine sample's sanctions list. The engine reads the trade's opaque marking only for an owner that is not on that list, so the row's No for the opaque marking is not a finding

    The ruleOwnership link: a counterparty whose owner is on the engine sample's sanctions list is Critical; otherwise an owner that the trade's own record marks as opaque is High; otherwise Low. The engine reads the opaque marking only for an owner that is not on that list. Engine band: Critical. This component carries 20% of the score: 18 points

  6. The owner behind the reference

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's row for this trade holds the counterparty's owner only as the reference EDES1. It does not hold the owner's name

    The ruleWhat the engine sample's trade file holds about the counterparty's owner besides its reference and the opaque marking: no name. The sample's sanctions list holds a name, listed_name, for an owner that is on it, which the engine does not read and its output does not hold

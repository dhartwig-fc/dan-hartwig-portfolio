FND005 · Network Intelligence

Investigation Workflow Intelligence

Of everything the platform has produced, what should an investigator open first, and why that?

FND005 Investigation Workflow Intelligence is the foundation capability that converts scored intelligence products into a ranked work queue, combining risk, confidence, exposure, materiality and complexity into a priority score, a band, named drivers and a queue assignment.

FND004 answers how risky. FND005 answers what first. A high risk finding nobody can evidence and a moderate one that is fully evidenced are ordered differently here, on purpose.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Priority read as risk, where the bands share names with FND004 and mean otherwise
  • Exposure silently defaulted to the risk score, putting most of the weight on one number
  • Materiality defaulted to fifty, where the product is unmeasured rather than neutral
  • Confidence reported between zero and one on a zero to a hundred scale
  • Well evidenced moderate finding outranking a weakly evidenced severe one
  • High complexity raising priority, counting the cost of work as a reason to do it sooner
  • Critical priority carrying no risk driver in its list

Investigation actions

  1. Read the priority drivers alongside the band
  2. Treat a critical priority with no risk driver as driven by confidence, materiality or complexity
  3. Check whether the products carried their own exposure and materiality scores before relying on a ranking
  4. Confirm the products feeding the queue report confidence on the zero to one scale
  5. Read queue position as review order rather than as severity order
  6. Escalate a low confidence high risk item on its risk score rather than waiting for the ranking

Detection logic

Five weighted components are combined and capped at a hundred. The weights are defaults on the prioritisation weights object and are injectable at construction:

Risk 0.35

Confidence 0.20

Exposure 0.20

Materiality 0.15

Complexity 0.10

The weights sum to 1.00.

Confidence carries a positive weight, which is a stated design choice and the opposite of a triage model that surfaces the least certain items for adjudication. The better evidenced a finding, the sooner it is reviewed, so a high risk low confidence finding sits below a moderate well evidenced one. That ordering is intended, but it must be understood, because it is where this product most differs from FND004.

Confidence is normalised by multiplying any value at or below one by a hundred, because upstream products report confidence on both a zero to one and a zero to a hundred scale. The test is magnitude, not provenance, so a product legitimately reporting a confidence of one out of a hundred is rescaled to the maximum. Any product feeding this engine should report confidence on the zero to one scale, where the rule is unambiguous.

Where a product supplies no exposure score, exposure takes the value of the risk score. The risk figure then carries the risk weight and the exposure weight together, which is fifty-five percent of the priority, and the queue becomes over half an echo of one number. Nothing in the output records that this happened. Check whether the product carried an exposure score before reading the priority as a five component judgement.

Materiality behaves the same way more visibly. Absent, it contributes a neutral fifty, which is seven and a half points of priority awarded for having no materiality data at all.

Where no complexity score is supplied it is derived from network shape: size times three capped at forty, relationship count times one and a half capped at forty, and ownership depth times seven capped at twenty. Complexity is the cost of investigating, and it is weighted positively, so a complex network is prioritised higher rather than deferred as expensive.

Scores are banded and routed to a queue:

85 and above Critical, Priority 1 Investigation Queue

70 to 85 High, Priority 2 Investigation Queue

50 to 70 Medium, Standard Review Queue

30 to 50 Low, Monitoring Queue

Below 30 Very Low, No Immediate Action Queue

The bands are identical to FND004's. The same thresholds over a different quantity: a critical priority and a critical risk are not the same claim, and the shared vocabulary invites conflating them.

Priority drivers are emitted in evaluation order. Unlike FND003 and FND004 the list is neither sorted nor deduplicated, so its order is stable but carries no ranking:

Critical risk score, at eighty-five or above

High risk score, at seventy or above

High confidence finding, at eighty-five or above after rescaling

Large connected network, at ten entities or more

Complex ownership structure, at depth three or more

Connected high-risk entities, at one or more

High-value network intelligence product, by product type

Critical investigation priority, at eighty-five or above

How this pattern looks

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: the work queue the FND005 engine built from six candidate products in NEXUS's synthetic sample, shown through the item it ranked first, which NEXUS's adapter takes as the subject. The candidates are rows of NEXUS's own sample file, written to stand in for other typologies' products: their figures and subjects are the file's and not those products' own results (the file gives the Network Risk Assessment item a risk figure of 92, where that product's own committed run holds a risk score of 17, which is fc-03's 16.57 rounded by NEXUS's adapter). Every name is fictional. The engine ranks items for review, and priority is not risk. It returns no risk band and no risk score of its own: the only risk figures in the run are the ones NEXUS's sample file gave each item, which the engine echoes back. For each item it returns a priority score, a level in its own words (Critical to Very Low, which are not NEXUS's risk bands), named drivers and a queue. It reads the five figures an item supplies (at the item's top level, as NEXUS's adapter gives them, or in a nested place that the adapter does not use), the item's network figures and its product type, and passes the item's id, name and subject through without reading them. The points each input adds are not in the run: they are worked here, the figure the engine read times its weight, and they add to the engine's score. This is the engine's own output for one sample queue, not a customer case.

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine lists it, or the item supplied the figure and the engine read it
  • not found the engine lists none, or the item supplied none and the engine used a default: nothing here says the figure is low
  • not stated the engine's output does not hold it

  1. The item's place in the queue

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine lists FND004 (Meridian Holdings network) first of 6 items, with a priority score of 89.95 of 100, which its cut-offs put at Critical; it routes the item to the Priority 1 Investigation Queue

    The ruleQueue position: the engine gives each item a priority score from five inputs, each multiplied by a weight, the five added, rounded to two places and capped at 100, and lists the items highest score first, items with the same score keeping the order they were given in. It puts the score at a level in its own words, 85 or more Critical, 70 or more High, 50 or more Medium, 30 or more Low and below 30 Very Low, and the level in a queue: Critical in the Priority 1 Investigation Queue, High in the Priority 2 Investigation Queue, Medium in the Standard Review Queue, Low in the Monitoring Queue and Very Low in the No Immediate Action Queue. These are the engine's words for review priority, not NEXUS's risk bands, and as this page's doctrine says, a critical priority and a critical risk are not the same claim. The step is found where the engine lists the item first and not found where it lists it later. Which item is the run's subject is NEXUS's adapter's choice, the first, so for the subject this step is always found

    The ruleRisk: the engine reads the item's risk figure at a weight of 35%, and reads 0 where the item supplies none.     found

    What the engine statesFND004 supplied 0.95 as its confidence figure, which is 1 or below, so the engine multiplied it by 100 and read 95, at a weight of 20%; worked here, that is 19 points of its priority score

    The ruleConfidence: the engine reads the item's confidence figure at a weight of 20%, and reads 0 where the item supplies none. A figure of 1 or below is first multiplied by 100, so 0.95 and 95 read the same and a figure of exactly 1 reads as 100, the top of the scale: the test is the number's size, not the scale it was meant on. The weight is positive, so a better evidenced item is placed higher, not lower. The step is found where the item supplied a confidence figure and the engine read it, and not found where it supplied none. Found says only that the item supplied the figure; it says nothing about whether the figure is high. Every item in this queue supplies all five

    The ruleDrivers: the engine names a driver for each of these tests on the item's own figures, in this order, and its list is neither ranked nor sorted: a risk figure of 85 or more gives Critical risk score, and otherwise 70 or more gives High risk score; a confidence figure of 85 or more, after the rescaling above, gives High confidence finding; a network size of 10 or more gives Large connected network; an ownership depth of 3 or more gives Complex ownership structure; one or more connected high-risk entities gives Connected high-risk entities; a product type of NI007, NI008, NI009 or NI010 gives High-value network intelligence product; and a priority score of 85 or more gives Critical investigation priority.

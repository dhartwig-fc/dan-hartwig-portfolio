SAN003 · Sanctions

Transshipment

Transshipment Evasion is the routing of goods through an intermediary jurisdiction to disguise their true origin or destination and defeat origin-based sanctions controls. Cargo that could not move directly between two points is re-papered at a hub — a free zone, an entrepôt — so the declared trade route no longer reveals the sanctioned leg.

The finding is a routing finding: it lives in the mismatch between declared and true origin, the number of transshipment hops, and the presence of a restricted commodity, corroborated by maritime behaviour and the jurisdictions involved.

Risk indicators

Trade-Routing

  • Declared origin ≠ true origin
  • One or more transshipment hops through a known hub
  • Restricted / controlled commodity
  • Generic goods descriptions on high-value cargo

Maritime

  • AIS transmission gaps on the true leg
  • Ship-to-ship transfers near the hub

Jurisdiction

  • True origin in a sanctioned jurisdiction
  • Hub in a high-risk free zone

Counterparty

  • Counterparties carrying sanctions exposure or adverse media

Investigation actions

  1. Resolve the shipment, its declared and true origin, and the hub.
  2. Test declared origin against true origin for a mismatch.
  3. Count transshipment hops and identify the hub.
  4. Assess restricted-commodity status.
  5. Aggregate maritime behaviour (AIS gaps, STS transfers).
  6. Score jurisdiction exposure for the true origin and hub.
  7. Score counterparty exposure.
  8. Aggregate; escalate at or above the threshold.

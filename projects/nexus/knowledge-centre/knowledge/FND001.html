FND001 · Network Intelligence

Entity Resolution

Which of these records describe the same party, and how confident are we?

And its consequence, which is the one an investigator acts on: does the risk recorded against one representation belong to this customer?

FND001 Entity Resolution is the foundation capability that resolves duplicate representations of one real-world entity into a single canonical identity, so that risk attaching to any representation attaches to all of them.

Without it, an entity appearing as Alpha Trading Ltd, ALPHA TRADING LIMITED and Alpha Trading is three parties carrying a third of the risk each, and no typology can see the aggregate. Every network product downstream is a statement about edges between entities, and is only as sound as the identity of the nodes.

A typology asks whether behaviour is suspicious. FND001 asks the question that must already be answered before that one means anything: are these records the same party?

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Unresolved duplicate understating exposure across every downstream product
  • Name gate cleared but the match carried thereafter by address alone
  • Name gate cleared but the match carried thereafter by telephone alone
  • Names align while registration numbers differ
  • Address shared by many otherwise dissimilar names
  • Merge uniting entities incorporated in different registries
  • Match score near a confidence band boundary

Investigation actions

  1. Read the matched attributes rather than the score alone
  2. Confirm the name gate was cleared before relying on a supporting attribute
  3. Treat a match supported only by a shared address as a co-location finding
  4. Re-read every typology finding against both representations once a match is accepted
  5. Record the reason whenever a match is rejected
  6. Treat an unresolved pair as a standing understatement of exposure, not a closed question

Detection logic

FND001 scores a candidate pair by weighted attribute agreement. The weights are governed constants in the entity resolution engine:

Name similarity 0.40

Registration number 0.30

Address 0.15

Email address 0.10

Telephone 0.05

The name gate is the load-bearing rule. Registration, address, email and telephone contribute only when name similarity already exceeds 0.85. A shared address between two dissimilar names is not evidence they are the same party. It is evidence of a shared address, which is a different product entirely — PAT005 Common Address. Removing that gate would collapse the two capabilities into one and make every serviced-office tenant a duplicate of every other.

The score is capped at 1.00 and rounded to four decimal places.

Name similarity is token-aligned and suffix-insensitive. Each token of one name is aligned to its best Jaro-Winkler counterpart in the other, the comparison is run in both directions, and the lower of the two totals is normalised by the longer token count. Taking the minimum is what makes the measure symmetric: a short name contained inside a longer one cannot score as a strong match on the strength of the generous direction alone.

The consequence is deliberate and worth stating, because it looks like a miss. Gammavane Imports Ltd and Gammavane Imports Limited resolve at 1.00, since corporate suffixes are stripped before comparison and a typo is tolerated. But Omega Trading and Omega Oil Trading fall below the gate, and so do Theta Investments and Theta Investment Holdings. An extra distinguishing word is penalised by design — it is usually a different company, not a spelling of the same one.

How this pattern looks

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: one record of NEXUS's own synthetic sample file of ten company records from three source systems, and the pairs fc-03's entity resolution engine returned for it. Every company is fictional. The engine compares every two records and returns the pairs that score 0.80 or more, each with a score and a confidence level in its own words; it returns no cluster, no canonical record and no band, so each step says only whether the engine's pairs hold something for the subject. Grouping the pairs into clusters, and choosing the record with the fullest name as the subject, are NEXUS's adapter's. This page's detection logic says registration, address, email and telephone contribute only when name similarity already exceeds 0.85: the engine does not do that. It credits each of them whatever the names, and it credits the name itself only above 0.85, so a pair whose names are too different to be credited (similarity 0.85 or less, but at least 0.50) can still be returned on the other four attributes: it takes a name similarity of at least 0.50, because 0.40 times 0.50 plus the other four attributes' 0.60 reaches the 0.80 threshold. So the name gate the doctrine describes is the doctrine's rule, not this engine's. The run holds the engine's pairs and the names the case gives, and not the records themselves, so no registration number, address, email or phone is shown. This is the engine's own output for one sample record, not a customer case.

Alpha Trading Limited · REG-2001 · pairs returned with it: 2 · the engine gives no band

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine's output holds it for the subject
  • not found the engine looked and its output holds none
  • not stated the engine's output does not say, or its figure is zero here

  1. Pairs returned for this record

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output holds 2 pairs with the subject at or above 0.80: Alpha Trading Ltd (CRM-1001) at score 1, confidence level Very High; Alpha Trading (PAY-3001) at score 1, confidence level Very High. The engine returns pairs, not clusters. NEXUS's adapter labels each record in the case with its source system in brackets; the names here are the records' own

    The rulePairs: the engine compares every two records of its sample file once and returns each pair whose score is 0.80 or more, with the score rounded to four places, a confidence level (Very High from 0.90, High from 0.75, Medium from 0.60, otherwise Low) and the attributes it credited. A pair under 0.80 is not returned, so the engine's silence about two records is not a finding that they differ. The step is found where the engine returned a pair with the subject at one end

  2. Name credited

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine credited the name in 2 of the subject's 2 pairs. No pair of the subject lacks it

    The ruleName: the engine strips legal suffixes (Ltd, Limited and others), anything in brackets and punctuation and lower-cases both names, aligns each word of each name to its best match in the other, adds the matches up in both directions, takes the smaller total and divides it by the larger word count, and credits the name only when that similarity is above 0.85. The engine adds 0.40 times the similarity to the score whether or not the name is credited: the credit is only the label in the pair's list. The similarity itself is not returned. The step is found where the name is credited in a pair with the subject and not found where it is credited in none of them

  3. Registration number credited

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine credited the registration number in 2 of the subject's 2 pairs. No pair of the subject lacks it

    The ruleRegistration number: the engine credits it, and adds 0.30, when both records give one and the two are exactly equal as written (capital letters and spaces count). It does not need the names to be alike. The step is found where it is credited in a pair with the subject and not found where it is credited in none of them

  4. Address credited

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine credited the address in 2 of the subject's 2 pairs. No pair of the subject lacks it

    The ruleAddress: the engine compares the two addresses with its NAME similarity (suffixes and punctuation stripped, lower-cased) and credits the address, adding 0.15, when that is above 0.90. It does not need the names to be alike. The step is found where it is credited in a pair with the subject and not found where it is credited in none of them

  5. Email credited

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine credited the email in 2 of the subject's 2 pairs. No pair of the subject lacks it

    The ruleEmail: the engine credits it, and adds 0.10, when both records give one and the two are equal once capital letters are ignored. It does not need the names to be alike. The step is found where it is credited in a pair with the subject and not found where it is credited in none of them

  6. Phone credited

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine credited the phone in 2 of the subject's 2 pairs. No pair of the subject lacks it

    The rulePhone: the engine credits it, and adds 0.05, when both records give one and the two are exactly equal as written (a leading space counts). It does not need the names to be alike. The five weights add to exactly 1.00, so the cap at 1.00 never binds. The step is found where it is credited in a pair with the subject and not found where it is credited in none of them

  7. The matched records also match each other

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output also holds a pair between records linked to the subject: Alpha Trading Ltd (CRM-1001) and Alpha Trading (PAY-3001) at score 1, confidence level Very High

    The rulePairs among the subject's partners: the engine compares every two records, so when the subject matches two records those two are also compared with each other. The step is found where the engine returned a pair between two records of which at least one is linked to the subject and neither is the subject, and not found where it returned none. The engine returns pairs only: the cluster these records make is NEXUS's adapter's

  8. What the engine does not read

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's output says nothing about the records' country, entity type or source system. The run lists the source systems of the subject's cluster, as NEXUS's adapter built it: COMPANIES_HOUSE, CRM, PAYMENTS. The engine does not know them

    The ruleWhat the engine does not read: its score reads a record's name, registration number, address, email and phone and nothing else about it. It does not read the record's country, its entity type or its source system, so two records from different systems, or one recording the country as UK and another as GB, are scored on the five attributes alone. This step can be neither found nor cleared

The engine returned 6 pairs across the 10 records, 2 of them with the subject. It gives no band and no alert line. The run's confidence figure, 1, is NEXUS's adapter's mean of the engine's scores over all 6 pairs, not a measure of the subject. 7 of the 8 steps found something; 0 found nothing and 1 could not be stated from the engine's output.

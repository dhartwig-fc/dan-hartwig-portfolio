FND004 · Network Intelligence

Network Risk Assessment

How risky is this entity network, and which part of it carries the risk?

The second clause is the product. A score without its components is a verdict without a reason, and this engine is built to return both.

FND004 Network Risk Assessment is the foundation capability that combines five weighted risk components into a single network risk score, a band, a set of named drivers, and a confidence figure derived from the evidence underneath.

FND001 says who the nodes are, FND002 what connects them, FND003 who stands above them. FND004 is the arithmetic that turns those three into a number an investigator can be asked to act on.

Risk indicators

Risk

  • Component reading exactly zero, which may mean unsupplied rather than absent
  • Sanctions exposure edge diluted by volume into the relationship mean
  • Ownership edge typed OWNS, PARTIAL_OWNER_OF, SHAREHOLDER_OF or ULTIMATE_OWNER_OF
  • Confidence reported as one over evidence that carried no confidence at all
  • High structural score on a large but otherwise ordinary network
  • Ownership depth at three or above, where the component is already capped
  • Score sitting just below a band boundary

Investigation actions

  1. Read the risk components before the aggregate score
  2. Treat a component reading exactly zero as unsupplied until confirmed absent
  3. Act on a sanctions or control driver rather than on the score that diluted it
  4. Check the confidence figure against whether the source edges carried scores
  5. Check whether an unscored ownership type would move a score near a band boundary
  6. State explicitly that the weights are uncalibrated defaults whenever they are argued over

Detection logic

Five components are combined by weight. The weights are defaults on the prioritisation weights object and are injectable at construction, so they are the governed starting point rather than a law of the model:

Entity risk 0.25, the mean risk score across entities

Relationship risk 0.20, the mean of type base score multiplied by edge confidence

Ownership risk 0.25, per path depth and stake multiplied by path confidence

Exposure risk 0.20, additive indicator scores plus connected high risk count

Structural risk 0.10, network size, edge count, ownership depth and density

The weights sum to 1.00 and the total is capped at 100.

Relationship risk assigns a base score by relationship type:

SANCTIONS_EXPOSURE 100

ULTIMATE_CONTROLLER_OF 90

CONTROLS 85

BENEFICIAL_OWNER_OF 80

TRADES_WITH and TRANSACTS_WITH 60

DIRECTOR_OF and SHARES_CONTACT_WITH 55

SHARES_ADDRESS_WITH 50

Any other type 40

Four of FND003's seven ownership types are missing from that list. OWNS, PARTIAL_OWNER_OF, SHAREHOLDER_OF and ULTIMATE_OWNER_OF all fall to the default of forty, which is below a shared address at fifty. An entity connected by a plain ownership edge therefore scores lower on relationship risk than one sharing a serviced office. Read the relationship component with the actual edge types in hand, because where they are these four the component understates.

Relationship risk is a mean, so volume dilutes severity. One sanctions exposure edge at a hundred among nineteen ordinary edges at forty yields a component of forty-three. The categorical finding survives in the drivers list and disappears from the number, which is why the drivers must be read alongside the score.

Ownership risk takes depth multiplied by twenty and capped at sixty, adds twenty-five where effective ownership reaches fifty percent or fifteen where it reaches twenty-five, multiplies the result by the path confidence and caps it at a hundred. Depth dominates: three hops already reaches the cap, so a deeper chain scores no higher than a three-deep one, and depth contributes more than a majority stake does.

Exposure risk is additive and capped at a hundred. A sanctions indicator adds forty, a PEP indicator twenty-five, adverse media twenty, a high risk indicator twenty, and anything else ten. Connected high risk entities add fifteen each up to a ceiling of forty-five.

Structural risk is pure shape: size times three capped at thirty, edges times one and a half capped at thirty, ownership depth times ten capped at twenty-five, and density times a hundred capped at fifteen. A large network scores high here regardless of conduct, which is why this component carries the smallest weight of the five.

An empty component scores zero and dilutes the total. A network with no ownership paths scores zero on a component weighted a quarter, which is indistinguishable from a network with fully evidenced benign ownership. Absence of data reads as absence of risk, and this is the most consequential property of the model.

Confidence is the mean of every relationship and ownership path confidence in the context. An absent confidence score defaults to one, so evidence that never stated its own confidence is counted as certain, and a context of wholly unscored edges reports the maximum confidence on the weakest possible footing.

Scores are banded as follows:

85 and above Critical

70 to 85 High

50 to 70 Medium

30 to 50 Low

Below 30 Very Low

Drivers are emitted sorted and deduplicated, and are the explainable half of the output. A driver states that a condition holds, not that it dominated the score: a control relationship driver fires on one such edge in a hundred.

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: the network the FND004 engine assessed in the toolkit's synthetic sample data, shown through the node NEXUS's adapter chose as its subject. Every person and company is fictional. The engine scores the whole network and is not told a subject. It returns five weighted components, one score, a risk-level word and a list of named risk drivers, and it holds no alert line; the run keeps neither the word nor the list, so each step says only whether the engine's figure for it is above zero. A figure of zero is never read as safe: three of the five components are zero here because the engine was handed nothing to score for them, and this page's detection logic says such an empty component is indistinguishable from a network with fully evidenced benign ownership. The run's own risk-driver sentences are written by NEXUS's adapter; for this network the engine's own list of drivers is empty. Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine's figure for it is above zero
  • not found not used here: a zero is never read as clear, because this adapter hands the engine nothing for three components
  • not stated the engine's figure is zero: nothing here says the network is clear

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives entity risk 0 for the network, at a weight of 25%, which is 0 points of the run's score

    The ruleEntity risk: the engine reads a risk score from each entity record, a record with no such column reading as 0 (a blank value stops the engine), and takes the mean over all entities.   3. Relationship risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives relationship risk 52.83 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 10.566 points of the run's score

    The ruleRelationship risk: the engine gives each relationship a base figure by its type (ultimate controller 90, control 85, beneficial owner 80, trades with or transacts with 60, director of or shares contact with 55, shares address with 50, sanctions exposure 100, any other type 40), multiplies it by that relationship's own confidence and takes the mean over all relationships, so many ordinary relationships dilute a severe one. The engine's sample relationship file holds 20 relationships, every one of the director type, with confidences averaging 0.9605, so this component is 55 times 0.9605, which the engine rounds to two places, 52.83. A file with no confidence column gives every relationship a confidence of 1, which the graph builder and the risk engine each default. A blank confidence in a row stops the graph builder, and a row with the cell missing altogether stops it with a different error; either way the run ends before the risk engine is reached. The engine does not check that a confidence figure lies between 0 and 1: a figure above 1 takes this component above 100 and one below 0 takes it below zero, and this walk refuses to describe a component outside 0 to 100. Every relationship scores at least 40 times its own confidence, so this figure is above zero for any network with a relationship of some confidence: found here says the network has relationships, not that any is severe. The run holds no relationship's type or confidence. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero

  5. Exposure risk

    not stated

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives exposure risk 0 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 0 points of the run's score

    The ruleExposure risk: the engine adds a figure for each exposure indicator it is handed, found by looking in the indicator's text, written in capitals, for the letters SANCTION (40), then PEP (25), then ADVERSE (20), then HIGH RISK (20), the first match winning and any other text scoring 10. It matches letters, not meanings: 'Politically exposed person' scores 10, 'High-risk jurisdiction' scores 10 and 'Not sanctioned' scores 40. It adds 15 for each connected high-risk entity the network figures report, up to 45, capped at 100. NEXUS's adapter hands it an empty list of indicators, and the network figures the analytics engine returns carry no count of connected high-risk entities, so this component is 0 in every run of this adapter. The per-entity exposure scores the analytics engine also returns (ten points per linked entity) are a different figure that the risk engine does not read. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A zero here is not a finding that the network has no exposure

  6. Structural risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives structural risk 60 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 10%, which is 6 points of the run's score

    The ruleStructural risk: the engine adds three points for each node up to 30, one and a half for each relationship up to 30, ten for each level of ownership depth up to 25, and 100 times the network's density up to 15, capped at 100. Of the four figures the engine reads, the network figures NEXUS hands it hold only the node count and the edge count, and no ownership depth or density, so in a run of this adapter this component cannot exceed 60, and it reaches 60 at 10 nodes and 20 links in the graph; the graph builder leaves out a relationship row whose source or target is blank. The first part scores at least 3 for any network with a node, so this figure is above zero for any network with a node: found here says the network has size. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure at that ceiling says the network is that large, not that it is risky

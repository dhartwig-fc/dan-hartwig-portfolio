PAT009 · Network Intelligence

Related Party Trading

Are trading counterparties connected through ownership, control or network relationships?

Supporting questions:

Do trading entities share ownership?

Do trading entities share directors?

Do trading entities share addresses?

Do trading entities share contacts?

Does the relationship appear disclosed?

Does the relationship increase financial crime risk?

NI009 Related Party Trading identifies trading activity occurring between entities that are connected through ownership, control, directors, addresses, contacts or other network relationships.

The purpose of this intelligence product is to detect:

  • Undisclosed related-party trading
  • Artificial trade activity
  • Trade-based financial crime
  • Circular trade arrangements
  • Transfer pricing risk
  • Sanctions exposure
  • Trade network manipulation

NI009 represents one of the most important bridges between FC-03 Network Intelligence and FC-04 TBML Analytics.

How this pattern looks

Engine sample · synthetic data

A worked example, step by step: one finding of fc-03's related-party-trading analytic over its own synthetic sample network of 9 entities and 8 relationships, and the records the engine read for it. Every person and company is fictional. The engine's rule: for each trade record it looks for a party that has a record of one of five types, beneficial owner, director, control, shares address with or shares contact with, against both trading parties, and returns a finding with one indicator for each such party. So to this engine a director is a controller, and so is a party that only shares an address; the party must be the source of both records, and it reads no percentage and no date. This page's questions ask whether the relationship appears disclosed and whether it increases financial crime risk: the engine cannot say either. It then scores each finding with a network risk engine; four of the five figures behind that score describe the whole sample network and not the trade, and two are zero only because the analytic hands the engine nothing for them. The records shown are the engine's INPUT, copied from its file. Each step says only whether the engine's output holds what its rule describes, or a figure above zero. The engine gives no alert line. The subject is the trade's source, the party NEXUS's adapter took as the case's subject. This is the engine's own output for one sample trade, not a customer case.

Each step: what the engine states, and the rule behind it

  • found the engine's output holds it for the subject

    What the engine statesThe engine's output holds a related-party finding for the trade between Kestrel Commodities Ltd (C001) and Kestrel Shipping Ltd (C002), with 1 indicator: Shared controller P001. The engine's input holds the trade record: Kestrel Commodities Ltd (C001) trades with Kestrel Shipping Ltd (C002), recorded in the file as TRADES_WITH, confidence 0.94

    The ruleRelated party trade: the engine takes every record whose type is trades with, transacts with, pays or receives payment from, and returns a finding for each whose two parties have a shared controller (the next step). It keeps the trading parties as the record gives them, source and target. It reads neither the amount, the date, nor the confidence of the trade record. The step is found where the engine returned a finding for the subject's trade. The subject is always one of the engine's own findings, so this step is found for every finding the walk shows

  2. The shared controller

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine names the party behind each indicator, and its input holds that party's records against both trading parties. P001 (the run holds no name for it), whose records against the two trading parties are: P001 (the run holds no name for it) is a director of Kestrel Commodities Ltd (C001), recorded in the file as DIRECTOR_OF, confidence 0.99; P001 (the run holds no name for it) is a director of Kestrel Shipping Ltd (C002), recorded in the file as DIRECTOR_OF, confidence 0.98. The engine counts a record of any of the five types as control

    The ruleShared controller: for each trade the engine collects every party that has a record of one of five types, beneficial owner, director, control, shares address with or shares contact with, against one of the two trading parties, and returns one indicator for each party that has such a record against both, labelled Shared controller and its id. A director therefore counts as a controller, and so does a party that only shares an address or a contact. It requires the party to be the SOURCE of both records, so a record running from a trader to the party does not count; it reads no percentage and no date, and the trade's own direction plays no part in that test. The records shown are what the engine read, not something it found. The step is found where the engine returned an indicator and the input holds the records of that party against both traders. The subject is always one of the engine's own findings, so this step is found for every finding the walk shows

    not stated

    The ruleEntity risk: the engine reads a risk score from each entity record of the whole network it is given, a record with no such column reading as 0 (a blank value stops the engine), and takes the mean over all entities. The toolkit's entity file for this sample has no risk score column, so every entity reads 0 and this component is 0 whoever the entities are. It describes the whole network and not the subject, and every finding of this sample run carries the same figure. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A zero here means nothing was supplied, not that the entities are clean

  4. Relationship risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives relationship risk 57.98 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 11.596 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleRelationship risk: the engine gives each relationship of the whole network a base figure by its type (ultimate controller 90, control 85, beneficial owner 80, trades with or transacts with 60, director of or shares contact with 55, shares address with 50, sanctions exposure 100, any other type 40; the engine's table spells the two shares kinds SHARES_CONTACT_WITH and SHARES_ADDRESS_WITH, so a record spelled SHARES_CONTACT or SHARES_ADDRESS reads as any other type), multiplies it by that relationship's own confidence and takes the mean over all of them, so many ordinary relationships dilute a severe one. The sample's 8 relationships are 3 DIRECTOR_OF (55), 2 BENEFICIAL_OWNER_OF (80), 2 TRADES_WITH (60) and 1 PAYS, a type the engine's table does not list (40); weighted by their own confidences the mean is 57.98. It reads every relationship of the file and not the finding's own, so every finding of this sample run carries the same figure. A blank confidence value stops the engine, and a missing confidence counts as 1. Every relationship scores at least 40 times its own confidence, so this figure is above zero for any network with a relationship of some confidence: found here says the network has relationships, not that any is severe. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero

The analytic hands the engine no list of ownership paths, so this component is 0 in every run of this analytic.   6. Exposure risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives exposure risk 10 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 20%, which is 2 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleExposure risk: the analytic hands the engine the finding's own indicator labels as its exposure indicators, and the engine reads the text of each: it adds 40 if the text contains SANCTION, otherwise 25 if it contains PEP, otherwise 20 if it contains ADVERSE or HIGH RISK, and otherwise 10 (capital letters are ignored, and a name that merely contains those letters counts: a name containing PEP would score 25), then 15 for each connected high-risk entity the network figures report, up to 45, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and report no connected high-risk entity. The finding's one label, Shared controller and an id, contains none of those words, so the engine adds 10 for it. Unlike the other network walks this figure depends on the finding: the engine adds a figure for each label, so a finding with two indicators would score 20 (every finding of this sample run has one). The run holds those labels. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the engine was handed something to read, not that anything about the subject is exposed

  7. Structural risk

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output gives structural risk 39 of 100 for the network, at a weight of 10%, which is 3.9 points of the engine's score for this finding

    The ruleStructural risk: the engine adds three points for each node up to 30, one and a half for each relationship up to 30, ten for each level of ownership depth up to 25, and 100 times the network's density up to 15, capped at 100. The analytics engine's network figures hold a node count and an edge count and no network size, ownership depth or density (it also returns lists of components, clusters, bridges, influence and exposure, which the risk engine does not read), so only the first two parts can score. This sample network has 9 nodes and 8 relationships, which the engine reads as 27 for the nodes (three each, under the cap of 30) and 12 for the relationships (one and a half each), 39 in all. It describes the whole network and not the subject, and every finding of this sample run carries it. The first part scores at least 3 for any network with a node, so this figure is above zero for any network with a node: found here says the network has size. The step is found where the engine's figure is above zero and not stated where it is zero. A figure here says the network is that large, not that it is risky

  8. The engine's own risk drivers

    found

    What the engine statesThe engine's output names 1 risk driver for this finding: Beneficial ownership relationship identified

    The ruleRisk drivers: the engine builds a sorted list of named drivers from the whole network and the finding: Beneficial ownership relationship identified when ANY relationship of the network is of that type, Control relationship identified when any is a control relationship, Sanctions exposure relationship identified when any is of that type, Layered ownership structure when it is handed an ownership path of depth 3 or more (none is here), Large connected network when the network figures give a network size of 10 or more (the analytics engine gives a node count and no network size, so this driver cannot appear for these runs), Connected high-risk entities when the figures count any (none), a Sanctions exposure or PEP exposure indicator when the finding's text contains sanction or pep, and Critical aggregate network risk at a score of 85 or more. The drivers about relationship types describe the whole network, not the subject. The step is found where the engine's list holds a driver and not found where it is empty
